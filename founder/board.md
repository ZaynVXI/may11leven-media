# Board summary: May11Leven Labs

**Vote:** 3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. **Average score:** 3.7 / 10 (Offers 3, Monopoly 4, Product 4).
The memos are in `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md` and `board/product.md`.

## Risks every member raised
1. **No proof.** There are no clients, results or testimonials. All three simulated panels said "it's a concept", and all three lenses put that first or second.
2. **Seven services, one buyer problem.** A small hotel wants more direct bookings. A menu of seven services hides that answer and spreads delivery thin.
3. **The price sells deliverables, not the outcome.** "Mark from $800" is a brand package, but the pain is commission. Buyers can't see what $800 buys or how it pays back.
4. **Missing numbers.** Nobody yet knows the market count, buyer budget, delivery hours, close rate or cost to win a client.

## Where they disagree
- **What the moat is.** For Offers, it's a guarantee and a price tied to commission saved. For Monopoly, it's a dataset that grows with every client: a direct-booking benchmark for hotels. For Product, it's a booking-and-deposit flow that guests trust as much as Booking.com.
- **How hard to cut.** Product would take the other services off the front page. Offers and Monopoly would keep them as add-ons.
- **Distribution.** Only Monopoly treats the no-outreach rule plus organic-only growth as a top-three killer. The other two don't raise it.

## Conditions checklist
- [ ] One lead offer for small hotels: a direct-booking package (site, WhatsApp and M-Pesa booking path, plus the brand pieces it needs), with one named measure (share of bookings that come direct). *(all three)*
- [ ] Price it against the commission it saves, not against design hours. *(Offers; test with /founder-pricing and /founder-cfo)*
- [ ] Write down exactly what's delivered, in what time, at what price, and what the client does. *(Product, Offers)*
- [ ] Add an affordable guarantee tied to something the studio controls, such as launching by a set day or working free until it does. *(Offers; cost it in /founder-cfo)*
- [ ] Win 1–3 real hotel projects, at a reduced rate if needed, with permission to publish before-and-after numbers. *(all three)*
- [ ] Test the booking-and-deposit flow end to end on a cheap phone and a slow connection. *(Product)*
- [ ] Name the moat to build, such as a hotel direct-booking benchmark from client data, shared only with permission. *(Monopoly)*
- [ ] Collect the missing numbers: hotels per market, buyer budget, delivery hours per package, close rate, cost to win a client. *(all three; /founder-competitors, /founder-consumer, /founder-cfo)*
- [ ] Keep the other six services as add-ons until the lead offer has proof. *(Offers, Monopoly)*

## The strongest version the board can see
Not quite what was pitched. The board can see **a direct-booking studio for independent hotels in East Africa**, one that:
- builds the brand, site and WhatsApp and M-Pesa booking path a small hotel needs to win guests back from the platforms;
- prices it against the commission saved;
- proves it with published before-and-after numbers from a few first clients.

The local know-how (cheap phones, slow connections, mobile money) is the edge. A benchmark that grows with every hotel could become the moat. Once the first results are out, the other services (automation, agents, social content, 3D) become upsells to the same clients.

This conflicts with the founder's standing rule to market every service, not just one. The board isn't saying drop the services. It's saying lead with one proven offer. That call belongs to the founder.
