# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- There is a small market that could be owned first. Independent hotels, guesthouses and boutique stays in East Africa are a narrow group, and the pain is clear: the brief puts booking-platform commission at roughly 15–25% on guests who could book direct. Being the obvious studio for that one group is a believable starting monopoly.
- There is a possible secret. Designing around M-Pesa deposits, WhatsApp, cheap phones and slow connections is local knowledge that studios built for Europe and the US do not usually apply. If that turns into measurable gains in direct bookings, it is real know-how.
- A brand is the only durable advantage a studio like this can realistically build, and the founder is already investing in it with a strict premium look.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a crowded market with nothing proprietary.** Branding and web design compete with local agencies, Fiverr and Upwork freelancers, Canva and AI tools. Nothing here is ten times better on something that matters yet, and good taste can be copied. Without proof, this is a price fight the studio would lose.
2. **Seven services spreads it too thin to own anything.** Brand, web, apps, 3D, automation, agents and social content compete with seven sets of rivals at once. Owning a small market means one offer for one buyer, done better than anyone else.
3. **Distribution is unproven and limited.** There are no clients, there is a rule against unsolicited contact, and the plan depends on organic posting and referrals. Nobody can yet say how the first 100 hotels arrive or what each one costs. The only demand evidence is three simulated panels, each returning 0 buyers out of 10.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many independent hotels and guesthouses are there in Rwanda alone, and what share could you win in two years?
- What specific result can you deliver that is ten times better than a local agency? For example, how many points of commission moved to direct bookings, and over what period?
- How do the first 10 paying hotels find you, and what does each one cost to win?
- What stops a Nairobi agency copying your hotel package six months after you publish it?
- Which six services would you drop for the first year?
- What can grow with each client: a booking-commission dataset, a reusable direct-booking system, or referrals between hotels?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Narrow to one offer for one buyer, a direct-booking package for small East African hotels, with a named measure such as a direct-booking share before and after.
2. Win three real paying hotels, at a discount if necessary, and publish their before-and-after numbers.
3. Name the moat to build: a proprietary benchmark of hotel direct-booking performance, or a repeatable system that improves with every client.
4. Bring back the missing numbers: market count, cost to acquire a client, close rate and delivery hours per package.
