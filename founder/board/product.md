# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The one-line promise is clear: "Most businesses are better than they look." That passes the one-sentence test.
- The first niche is specific, and its pain is concrete and costly: small hotels lose roughly 15–25% commission to booking platforms on guests they could reach directly. A product can be built back from that.
- The design inputs come from real use: M-Pesa deposits, WhatsApp, cheap phones and slow connections. That is starting from the guest's experience, not from the studio's portfolio.
- Fixed, named packages with public "from" prices remove a seam most agencies leave open: the vague quote.
- The craft bar is high and written down, and self-initiated work is honestly labelled as concept.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** Seven services for a buyer who has one problem. A guesthouse owner wants more direct bookings, not brand, web, apps, 3D, AI agents and social. Seven offers make it harder for a buyer to see what the studio is for, and harder to deliver each one brilliantly with a small team. This is the root of the next two.
2. **The buyer cannot picture the result.** Every simulated panel's top objection was "it is a concept, not a live product." And "I cannot see what the $800 buys" means the first five minutes of the experience are not designed yet. With no client and no result, the experience exists only on paper.
3. **The weakest seam belongs to someone else.** "Guests trust Booking.com more than my own website." A beautiful site does not get the hotel paid if the booking, deposit and confirmation flow feels less safe than the platform. If that hand-off breaks, the craft upstream does not matter.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If only one service could exist for the first niche, which one, and what would you cut?
- Describe the hotel owner's first five minutes after paying. What do they get, see and do?
- What exactly does $800 buy: files, pages, hours, revisions?
- How does a guest book and pay a deposit on a site you build, and why would they trust it over Booking.com?
- How many delivery hours does each package take? (Open: not known.)
- What will the website and automation packages cost? (Open: not priced.)
- What measure will the first project be judged on, and how will you report it to the client?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Cut to one lead product for small hotels: a direct-booking website (brand included as needed), with one named measure, such as the share of bookings that come direct. Keep the other services off the front page until it works.
2. Write down, before selling, exactly what is delivered, in what time, at what price, and what the client does.
3. Ship one real, live project (even at a reduced price, honestly labelled) and publish the measured result.
4. Design and test the booking-and-deposit flow end to end on a cheap phone and a slow connection before calling the product finished.
