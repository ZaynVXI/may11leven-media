# Board brief: May11Leven Labs

# May11Leven Labs

- What it is: a minimal, premium creative tech studio offering seven services under one system: brand identity, websites, apps, packaging and 3D, AI automation, AI agents, and social content.
- Who it is for: owners and marketing leads of growing businesses that sell on looks and trust. First niche: small hotels, guesthouses and boutique stays in East Africa (Rwanda, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda), then real estate and interiors, then the Gulf, then anywhere.
- What it sells, at what price: fixed, named packages with public "from" prices. Approved: brand identity ("Mark") from $800. Website ("Storefront") and automation ("Night shift") packages exist but are not priced yet. A planned monthly retainer after launch. Entry offer: a free first look (a short diagnosis, never free design), requested by commenting "11" or by email.
- Where and how: online, remote delivery. Found through Instagram, LinkedIn (main business channel), Facebook, referrals and free directory listings. Work is shown through self-initiated concept projects (always labelled concept) and original research (e.g. a study of hotel websites, a booking-commission calculator).
- Budget and constraints: free tools only; any spending needs the founder's approval. No paying clients, testimonials or case results yet. A small founder-led team. Strict brand rules: premium minimal look benchmarked against studios such as Motto, Obys, .raw and Artemii Lebedev; quality over posting volume; never post AI-generated imagery; never contact leads unsolicited.

## More context a stranger needs
- Positioning: "Most businesses are better than they look." The studio promises work that looks premium and also does a business job (more direct bookings, more trust, less manual work).
- Differentiators claimed: one studio for brand, web, apps, 3D and AI so the system holds together; craft at the level of global studios with local knowledge of African and Gulf markets (M-Pesa deposits, WhatsApp, cheap phones and slow connections as design inputs); problem first, with a named measure for each project.
- The core pain for the first niche: small hotels pay roughly 15–25% commission to booking platforms on guests they could reach directly, and often look smaller online than they are.
- Competition as the studio sees it: local branding and web agencies, freelancers on Fiverr and Upwork, DIY tools such as Canva and AI generators, and simply relying on Instagram plus booking platforms.
- Evidence so far: three simulated buyer panels (language-model personas, not real customers) all returned 0 buyers out of 10. The top objection each time was trust: "It is a concept, not a live product… no client, no result." Others: "Guests trust Booking.com more than my own website" and "I cannot see what the $800 buys." These are simulations, not market data.
- Unknowns: market size, cost to acquire a client, close rate, delivery hours per package, prices for six of the seven services.
