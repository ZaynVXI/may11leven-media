# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The first niche has a pain you can name in money. Small hotels pay roughly 15–25% commission to booking platforms on guests they could reach directly. That outcome can be measured, and it is far stronger than "a nicer brand".
- The buyers are easy to find. Small hotels and guesthouses are public, listed and active on Instagram, LinkedIn and booking sites.
- The free first look is a sound way in. It is a diagnosis, not free design, so it costs little to deliver and shows expertise before money changes hands.
- Fixed, named packages with public "from" prices remove a lot of buying friction.
- The local design inputs (M-Pesa deposits, WhatsApp, cheap phones, slow connections) add real value for this buyer, and a generic freelancer is unlikely to offer them.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Zero likelihood.** No clients, no results and no testimonials, and every simulated panel rejected it for exactly this reason. Under this lens, perceived likelihood is the biggest lever, and right now it is close to nothing. Concept work does not prove that bookings went up.
2. **The offer sells deliverables, not the outcome.** "Mark from $800" is a logo package, and the buyer said they cannot see what the $800 buys. The pain is commission, but the priced product is identity. Nothing ties the price to more direct bookings, and there is no guarantee.
3. **An unproven market and a scattered stack.** Nobody knows whether small East African hotels have budget for $800 or more, and seven services spread the offer thin. A buyer who has one problem (commission) sees a menu, not a solution.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- How many direct bookings does a typical target hotel lose to platforms each month, and what is that worth in dollars? (Not yet known.)
- What do these owners spend on marketing or web work today, if anything?
- What exactly does a hotel get, step by step, from first look to first direct booking, and how long does it take?
- How many delivery hours does each package take, and what can you afford to guarantee?
- Why price identity first when the pain sits in bookings?
- What close rate and acquisition cost would make this work? (Both are open questions.)
- Could you charge double for an offer tied to booking results and still be the obvious choice?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Rebuild the lead offer around one outcome: a direct-booking package for small hotels (site, WhatsApp/M-Pesa booking path, the brand pieces it needs), priced against the commission it saves.
2. Add a guarantee you can afford, tied to something you control (for example, launch by a set day or keep working free).
3. Get proof before you scale. Run one to three real projects at a reduced rate in exchange for permission to publish results, and track direct bookings before and after.
4. Collect the missing numbers: buyer budget, delivery hours, close rate and acquisition cost.
5. Keep the other services as add-ons until the lead offer has proof.
