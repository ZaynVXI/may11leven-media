Five tests we run on every logo before we call it finished, our own included.

1. Shrink it to 16 px. That's the size of a browser tab or app icon. If it turns into a smudge, it fails.
2. Print it in one colour. A rubber stamp, an embroidered shirt, a receipt. No gradients, no shadows, no second colour to lean on.
3. Read it from across the street. A shop sign is seen from 10 metres, often from a moving car or motorbike.
4. Photocopy it. Fax, cheap receipt printers and WhatsApp compression all strip a mark down to black and white.
5. Look for three seconds, then draw it from memory. If people can't recall the shape, they won't recognise it next time.

Most logos we're shown pass two or three of these. That's rarely a design emergency, but it usually means the brand is working harder than it should.
