# Term: research (concept, digital product)

**Problem and user:** Kenyan parents pay school fees three times a year in one lump, and the lump arrives in January, May and September, often after holidays. Fees alone at many private schools run about KES 20,000–40,000 per child per term before transport and meals (The Star, Jan 2025). WorldRemit's study puts yearly education costs at roughly ten times average monthly household income, and Tala reports schooling as the top reason Kenyans borrow from digital lenders (both cited in The Star, Jan 2025). The economist in The Standard names the tension: daily needs against saving for fees. User: a parent with two children, paid monthly or weekly, using M-Pesa, checking the app at night after work.

**Product idea:** Term splits each child's next term fee into small weekly amounts, saves them in a locked pot that only pays the school's paybill, and shows one calm status: covered, on track, or behind and by how much. A receipt from the school closes the loop.

**Feeling (3 words):** calm, ahead, proud.

**Category products studied:**
- SchoolPay Savings (Airtel Money, SchoolPay, Sanlam, Uganda): save from as little as UGX 5,000, any time, fees paid straight from the savings wallet with no withdrawal charge. Lesson: the save-and-pay loop in one place is the value; we show it as one flow.
- Schoolable (Nigeria) and Edupali (Nigeria): plan, save and track fees. Lesson: tracking per child matters, so each child gets their own card.
- Furaha and TermFund: fee financing (loans). Avoid list: we design for saving ahead, never lending.

**Design references studied:**
- Refero styles: Wise "deep moss green with lime accents", Mercury "alpine banking at blue hour", Brex "white concrete, single ember accent". Lesson: serious money products own one material mood and one accent.
- Awwwards jury pages (Aston Currency Management, SOTD; Mercury): money sites score highest on clarity and calm motion, not decoration.
- Qclay Aura (brand/refs/qclay/17-aura-glass.jpg): data written as a sentence, one large word as label, blurred colour seen through glass.
- Holographik: each project keeps its own colour world.
- .raw: air, one focal point.
- Trends sweep (brand/learning/trends.md): glassmorphism with solid zones behind text; hyper-local colour.
- Limits: Mobbin needs a paid plan and Awwwards visuals don't load here; screen-level study used the references above.

**Visual language:** soft glass and aura. Different from the last product (After hours: warm editorial with real photos).

**Colour source:** Kenyan school uniforms: maroon jumper, bottle-green blazer, navy, sky-blue sweater, grey trousers, white shirt. Each child's card glows in their own school's uniform colour; the app frame stays chalk-white and graphite. One accent: chalk yellow for "covered".

**Type:** Geist for UI (clear numerals, tabular), with IBM Plex Mono for status pills. Reason: money needs unambiguous 1/l/I and tabular figures (our I, l, 1 test).

**Steal list:**
1. Qclay: sentence-as-data, transformed into school language ("Term 1 is covered.").
2. Holographik: colour world per child, not per brand.
3. Wise/Refero: one material mood plus one accent.

**Avoid list:** piggy banks, cartoon kids, pencils and books, purple gradients, loan language, round numbers, faces.
