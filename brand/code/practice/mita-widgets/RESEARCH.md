# Mita widgets: research (concept, not a client, not KPLC's product)

## The problem
A prepaid meter shows units left. People want to know when the power runs out and what their money bought. Mita (practice 10) already solved the second on the app's home screen; this piece moves the answer to the phone's home screen, so nobody has to open an app to read it.

## Truth we rely on (from practice 10's research)
- A KPLC unit is about KES 12, "about 16 with taxes"; up to 20% of a purchase can clear arrears first (peopledaily.digital, kenyans.co.ke).
- So KES 1,000 bought 35.2 kWh, split energy 683 / taxes and levies 317 / arrears 0, is the believable breakdown we used before.
- Concept data: 12.4 kWh left and a usual 3.1 kWh a day are the user's own readings (the app would compute the daily average from their history). 12.4 / 3.1 = 4.0 days; after a KES 1,000 top-up 12.4 + 35.2 = 47.6 kWh, 47.6 / 3.1 = 15.4, so "about 15 days" and "two weeks".

## Category look (what we saw)
- PXDX widget wall (brand/refs/pxdx-widget-wall): square tiles, one value lit per tile, a sentence tile with only the values in white, values change in place.
- iOS widget sizes: small 158 x 158 pt, medium 338 x 158 pt; a small widget is one tap target, so no buttons inside it.
- Safaricom's My OneApp consolidation (techcabal.com, Apr 2026): one app for many jobs makes the home-screen reading more valuable.

## Look and feel
- Feeling: calm, informed, ahead of the outage.
- Language: Mita's own LCD utility system (Bone frame, ink tiles, one LCD green, Archivo condensed numerals, Plex Mono labels), placed on a dark home screen. The last piece (coach seat picker) was Swiss data on paper.
- Steal list (thinking, not surface): PXDX widgets (values lit inside a sentence, swap in place); Qclay (fragment framing: a cropped screen, no full device); Holographik price ladder (one value lifted, the rest set back by tone); Motto workbook (labels in the frame corners, the structure is the cover); Nuevo.Tokyo (the number is the image).
- Avoid: dials and gauges, gradient wallpapers, emoji, a nested "Top up" button, the same number twice.
