# Tuma (name check) research

Problem and user: sending mobile money to the wrong number in Kenya. Safaricom's Hakikisha shows the recipient's name before a payment; the first version (2015) asked people to dial 1 within 15 seconds to cancel, and many didn't understand it. The redesign replaced that with an explicit Yes / No step, and daily reversal requests fell from about 12,000 to 4,000 (techcabal.com, "Safaricom's big fix"; standardmedia.co.ke "Hakikisha"). Lesson: the confirm step works when the name is the biggest thing and "no" is as easy as "yes".
User: anyone paying a person or a till in Kenya, often one-handed, often outside in bright light.
Feeling (3 words): sure, calm, unhurried.
Category screens studied: Hakikisha prompt as described in press (name in capitals, Yes / No); Wise send-money review step (wise.design: the amount and the recipient on one card before the button); Apple Pay confirm (one action, the state change is the reward).
Award/gallery sites studied: Dribbble Select 2026 "Best Shots of the Year" (calmer fintech, restrained execution; EasyPay by Nickelfox, insurance app by Paperpillar); hontran.dev award list 2026 (guided walk-through, one thing at a time); Qclay aura (brand/refs/qclay/17-aura-glass.jpg, data as a calm sentence); PXDX player experiment (brand/refs/pxdx-player/); PXDX podcast UI details (brand/refs/pxdx-dribbble/podcast-ui-details.jpg).
Zayn's references used: pxdx-player (one component, numbered variants), qclay/17 (aura glass for money), lebedev-02 (one question per card, answer under an arrow).
Visual language: Soft glass and aura. Last piece (Ikiyaga) was warm editorial; before that Mita (LCD utility) and Zanzibar (Swiss data).
Colour and material: Nairobi in November, when the jacarandas bloom: jacaranda violet aura on a dusk ink ground. Violet is the one accent.
Type: Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (built for low-vision reading; the name and the amount must never be misread: 0/O, 1/l), Geist Mono for phone numbers and codes.
Avoid list: M-Pesa green or any real operator's colours and marks; a countdown that pressures people; hiding "Not them" as a grey text link.
Concept only, not a client. No real person: the name is invented, the number is masked.
