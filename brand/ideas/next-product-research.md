## The challenge (design-challenge-framer)
**How might we help small businesses that post photos of people (lounges, salons, gyms, schools, event hosts) share those photos only with a clear yes, without slowing down the night or the school day?**
- In scope: venues, salons and barbers, gyms, private schools, event hosts and the photographers they hire. Kenya first, then Nigeria, South Africa, Rwanda, then the Gulf (UAE, Saudi), then anywhere.
- Out: legal advice (we point to a lawyer), storing biometrics, enterprise DAM systems.
- Success: a business can show who said yes to which photo, guests get their photos, and nothing goes up with a face that said no.

## Recommendation: Nod (working name)
**One line:** a yes before the post.

**Why it's right for us**
- It solves a real, documented problem with real fines, in all three of our markets (Africa, Gulf, world).
- It sits on our own work: social content is one of our services, and every brand we post for needs this. We already never use faces from reference material, so we practise what we sell.
- It uses our cybersecurity edge (data protection) and the founder's photography background (model releases are everyday work for photographers).
- It's a new sector and a new look for the feed: no hotels, no fintech.

**The pull, not just the push.** Fear of a fine is weak on its own (only 20 penalty notices in Kenya so far). The stronger reason: guests who say yes get their photos on WhatsApp, and people share photos of themselves. Premagic reports over 90% of attendees at one event registered to receive their own photos. That shows interest in receiving photos, not consent to let a venue publish them; whether a yes to publishing grows reach is a hypothesis to test separately (Codex review).

**What we'd build (all seven services, one world)**
| # | Service | Piece |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Brand | Nod: name, mark (a small dip, the nod), voice, palette, type |
| 02 | Website | Product site plus the free tool |
| 03 | App | Staff app: take or upload a photo, each face gets a status (yes, not me, not asked); "not me" and "not asked" stay blurred |
| 04 | Packaging and 3D | The physical side, rendered in Blender: table cards with a QR, a two-colour wristband or lanyard (a known event practice), a sticker for salon mirrors |
| 05 | AI automation | Parent consent forms on WhatsApp at the start of term, with renewals and a log the school can export |
| 06 | AI agent | Checks every post before it's scheduled: faces without a yes, children, names in captions; drafts the fix |
| 07 | Social content | The launch series, plus the guest's own gallery link |

**Signature move (PXDX bar):** blur until yes. A face stays soft until its owner says yes; then it sharpens. One motion, the whole idea, and it keeps faces out of our posts.

**The free working tool (answers "give me something I can try")**
Nod card: a printable table or mirror card with a QR. **As first drafted it was not real consent** (Codex review): a generic "photos are fine" message has no purpose, retention period or link to a photo, and the ODPC's recorded-media guidance says a notice isn't consent and consent forms must name the purpose, data, processing, withdrawal right and consequences. So the card either (a) opens a short, specific consent page (who is asking, which purpose such as "our Instagram tonight", how long it's kept, how to withdraw), or (b) is labelled honestly as a scan-interest test. Decide before any build.

3. Schools will run consent through WhatsApp. → One private school, one term.

## More evidence (second pass, 19 Nov)
- **The regulator already describes the product.** The ODPC's Guidance Note for the Education Sector (Feb 2024) says to get explicit parental consent before publishing any photo of a child, to have "procedures in place to demonstrate that an individual has consented", to let people withdraw consent, not to post photos with children's names, and to prefer crowd shots where children aren't identifiable. Nod's school flow is that checklist made usable.
- **It's not only guests and pupils.** In January 2025 the ODPC ordered a solar company to pay KES 500,000 to a former sales agent whose photo it used in marketing flyers without consent; the employer carries the burden of proof (EY, Oct 2025). Staff photos in marketing are a second use case. Caveat: a Nairobi court ruled in Nov 2026 that the ODPC can't award compensation, so treat compensation orders as uncertain; fines and enforcement notices are unaffected.
- **Teachers on TikTok.** Business Daily (May 2026) reports teachers risking legal action for posting classroom videos, and parents suing schools over children's images used in marketing.
- **Gulf.** Saudi Arabia's data authority issued 48 enforcement decisions in its first active year, many over marketing without consent (IAPP, Feb 2026). No image-specific cases found yet; the UAE photo law is the stronger Gulf angle.
- **Correction:** one source dates the school fine to 2024; Clyde & Co's contemporaneous report says the penalty notices were issued in late September 2023. We use 2023.

## Joint review with Codex (PR #34)
Codex reviewed the first pass and raised five points. All checked against the sources and accepted:
1. The card wasn't real consent. Fixed above; see the recorded-media guidance below.
2. Premagic's 90% is interest in receiving photos, not consent to publish. Reworded.
3. The registration fee: KES 4,000 to register, KES 2,000 to renew. Fixed.
4. Public copy said "one Instagram post"; the sources say social media (the case summary lists Instagram, Facebook and WhatsApp). Fixed.
5. The 29/30 score overstated the case: the complaint totals cover all data protection, not photo consent, and Nigeria's notices targeted finance, insurance and gaming. Nod rescored to 26 (reach 4, evidence 4, working tool 3). It still leads, but narrowly, so the decision rests on a real-world test, not the score.

Codex's alternative, bank-fraud defence, is real: Kenyan banks lost KES 1.9bn to fraud in 2025 (KES 1.59bn in 2024), 385 cases, and recovered only about 6% of the money exposed (CBK via TechTrends, Nov 2026). It scores lower for us as a studio concept: the buyer is a bank with long procurement and its own security vendors, and our earlier consumer scam-checker mockup tested 0/10. Kept on the shortlist as the consumer-education angle (our Check idea), built only as a working tool.

**The strongest new source (found by Codex):** the ODPC's Guidance Notes for Processing on Publications of Recorded Media (2025). It requires express consent before photographing identifiable people; a consent form separate from registration, naming the organisation, purposes, data, processing, withdrawal right and consequences; notice is not consent; at events, written notices plus clear opt-outs such as a designated area or stickers; images used only for the stated purpose; takedown from social media as soon as possible after withdrawal; a retention schedule with evidence of deletion; and parental consent for children, blurring faces where possible. Hospitality and educational institutions must register with the ODPC whatever their size (ODPC compliance card). This turns Nod from "nice to have" into the regulator's own checklist, and it gives us the product requirements:
- consent per purpose (tonight's Instagram, the website, a print ad), never a blanket yes
- an opt-out that works in a dark room: a sticker or wristband, as the guidance itself suggests
- one-tap withdrawal that triggers takedown tasks on every channel
- a retention clock and a deletion log
- children: parent consent, blur by default

## Joint shortlist (Claude's proposal, sent to Codex for agreement)
1. **Nod**, rescoped to the ODPC recorded-media requirements above. First real test: show the scoped consent flow (paper prototype) to five lounge, salon or school owners.
2. **Lapse**, website and domain watch: our own data, our security edge, easiest to ship as a real tool.
3. **Check**, consumer fraud defence: real losses (CBK), but only as a working tool, never a mockup.
