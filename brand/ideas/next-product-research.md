## The challenge (design-challenge-framer)
**How might we help small businesses that post photos of people (lounges, salons, gyms, schools, event hosts) share those photos only with a clear yes, without slowing down the night or the school day?**
- In scope: venues, salons and barbers, gyms, private schools, event hosts and the photographers they hire. Kenya first, then Nigeria, South Africa, Rwanda, then the Gulf (UAE, Saudi), then anywhere.
- Out: legal advice (we point to a lawyer), storing biometrics, enterprise DAM systems.
- Success: a business can show who said yes to which photo, guests get their photos, and nothing goes up with a face that said no.

## Six problems we researched
| # | Idea | The problem, with evidence |
|---|---|---|
| A | **Nod**: photo and content consent | Kenya's data regulator fined a Nairobi restaurant KES 1,850,000 for posting a guest's photo on social media without consent, and a school KES 4,550,000 for posting pupils' photos without parents' consent (penalty notices, Sept 2023). The business must be able to **prove** consent (Clyde & Co). By Jan 2026 the regulator had received 9,061 complaints, issued 134 enforcement notices and 20 penalty notices; about 28,000 organisations are registered (TechTrends, Nov 2026). In the UAE, photographing people and circulating the images without consent can carry fines of up to Dh500,000 (Federal Law 34 of 2021, Khaleej Times). Nigeria's regulator sent compliance notices to 1,368 firms in 2025 (mostly finance, insurance and gaming). |
| B | **Proof**: diaspora house-build checks | Many stories of money sent home for a house and lost (Yen, Briefly, Nation). But the 2025 Kenya remittances survey found only a small share of households put remittances into real estate (TechTrends, June 2026), and several platforms already exist (Urbex, Koles, a Sierra Leone tracker). |
| C | **Trace**: EUDR passport for coffee and cocoa | EU deforestation rules apply from 30 Dec 2026 for large firms and 30 June 2027 for small ones; many Kenyan coffee farmers struggle with plot geolocation (The Standard, Jan 2026). Real deadline, but crowded with funded players and long B2B sales. |
| D | **Viewing**: honest rentals in Nairobi | Fake listings and deposits taken for houses that don't exist, rising in Kilimani, Kileleshwa, Lavington (Citizen, Oct 2025). A marketplace needs both landlords and renters before it works. |
| E | **Lapse**: website and domain watch | Our own Field Study: about 1 in 6 listed hotel websites gone or taken over. Strong edge, but it shows only three of our services. |
| F | **Check**: scam-SMS checker | Real harm, but the mockup tested 0/10; it only works as a hosted live tool. |

## Recommendation: Nod (working name)
**One line:** a yes before the post.

**Why it's right for us**
- It solves a real, documented problem with real fines, in all three of our markets (Africa, Gulf, world).
- It sits on our own work: social content is one of our services, and every brand we post for needs this. We already never use faces from reference material, so we practise what we sell.
- It uses our cybersecurity edge (data protection) and the founder's photography background (model releases are everyday work for photographers).
- It's a new sector and a new look for the feed: no hotels, no fintech.

**The pull, not just the push.** Fear of a fine is weak on its own (only 20 penalty notices in Kenya so far). The stronger reason: guests who say yes get their photos on WhatsApp, and people share photos of themselves. Premagic reports over 90% of attendees at one event registered to get their photos. Consent becomes reach.

**What we'd build (all seven services, one world)**
| # | Service | Piece |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Brand | Nod: name, mark (a small dip, the nod), voice, palette, type |
| 02 | Website | Product site plus the free tool |
| 03 | App | Staff app: take or upload a photo, each face gets a status (yes, not me, not asked); "not me" and "not asked" stay blurred |
| 04 | Packaging and 3D | The physical side, rendered in Blender: table cards with a QR, a two-colour wristband or lanyard (a known event practice), a sticker for salon mirrors |
| 05 | AI automation | Parent consent forms on WhatsApp at the start of term, with renewals and a log the school can export |
| 06 | AI agent | Checks every post before it's scheduled: faces without a yes, children, names in captions; drafts the fix |
| 07 | Social content | The launch series, plus the guest's own gallery link |

**Signature move (PXDX bar):** blur until yes. A face stays soft until its owner says yes; then it sharpens. One motion, the whole idea, and it keeps faces out of our posts.

**The free working tool (answers "give me something I can try")**
Nod card: a business types its name and WhatsApp number and prints a table or mirror card with a QR. The QR opens WhatsApp with "Yes, photos of me are fine (table 4)" or "Please don't post me". Nothing is stored by us. It works on day one and costs nothing to host as a static page.

## DFV check (honest)
| Leg | Verdict | Evidence or gap |
|---|---|---|
| Desirability | **Untested** | Fines and laws show the problem; no real venue owner, school or guest has seen it yet |
| Feasibility | Supported | Face detection and blurring run on-device today; WhatsApp links need no backend; the card tool is static |
| Viability | Untested | No price test. Reference points: Kenya registration fees are KES 2,000 for small firms; the fines are KES 1.85–4.55M |

**Weakest leg:** desirability. Do venue owners care enough to change how they post?

## Riskiest assumptions and the cheapest test for each
1. Venue owners will ask guests for a yes without it feeling awkward. → Show the Nod card to five real lounge or salon owners (Zayn's call who; we never contact anyone ourselves).
2. Guests will scan to get their photos. → Count scans on one real night with the free card.
3. Schools will run consent through WhatsApp. → One private school, one term.
4. Enforcement keeps growing. → Watch the regulator's notices monthly (note: a Nairobi court ruled in Nov 2026 that the regulator can't award compensation, but fines and enforcement notices stand).

## How we market it (and ourselves)
1. Educational post: "A Nairobi restaurant was fined KES 1.85M for one Instagram post." (We don't name the business; it's public record, but shaming isn't our style.)
2. The Nod carousel, PXDX structure: hero, manifesto, the card, the app's blur-until-yes, the post check, the school flow, the Gulf version, offer.
3. The free Nod card tool, live.
4. LinkedIn: "What schools need before posting a photo of a child" (Kenya, South Africa POPIA, Nigeria NDPA), professional, with sources.
5. Offer: "Every brand we design gets a consent-ready content system." It sells social content, web and AI agents together.

## Open before we build
- Trademark and handle check on the name (founder-brand skill); Nod is a working name.
- Legal review of claims: we state laws and fines with sources and never give legal advice.
- Founder panel on the pitch before the first post (our standing rule).
- A real test needs real people; that's Zayn's call.
