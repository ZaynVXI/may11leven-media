# Motto and Lebedev: method, and how short they write

## Measured copy length
| | Lebedev (artemiilebedev.com) | Motto (wearemotto.com/services) |
|---|---|---|
| Tagline | "make meaning, not noise." 4 words | "Pick your path to progress." 5 words |
| Labels | 1–3 words, lowercase mono: work, archive, contact | Service names of 2 words: Brand Strategy, Brand Identity |
| Longest sentence | 26 words (about section) | 15 words (program description) |
| Headlines and CTAs | Name + role | 4–8 words ("Book a discovery call") |
| Whole page | Mostly titles, categories, years | ~500–700 words, each section a headline, one line and a list |
| Lists | client / industry / year | Named programs: FastTrack®, Foundation®, Flagship® |

## Our writing rules from this
1. Labels: 1–3 words. Headlines: 8 words or fewer. Body lines: 15 words or fewer.
2. One idea per line. If a sentence has a comma and an "and", split it or cut it.
3. Show with lists of facts (client · industry · year), not paragraphs.
4. Name the offers (Motto's ® programs). Ours need names too.
5. Keep the method in full, but in a separate, quiet place (a footnote or a "How it works" block).

## Lebedev's process (Codrops spotlight)
1. Get to know the founder or team first.
2. Brief thoroughly. Context drives quality.
3. Explore 4–5 concepts.
4. Find the one idea or metaphor ("Design without an idea simply exists").
5. Merge the best of the concepts into one result.
6. Present it as carefully as you designed it.
- Use proven patterns. Don't reinvent the wheel.
- Simplicity wins.
- Turn down work he doesn't like.
- Tools are a means: Figma, Photoshop, After Effects, C4D + Redshift, Webflow.

## Motto's process (DesignWanted interview, services page)
1. Immersion: leadership and culture.
2. Brand strategy: the "Idea Worth Rallying Around®".
3. Verbal identity: messaging, story, voice.
4. Visual identity: style boards, explore, then prototypes on real applications.
- Research informs, intuition decides.
- Slow down to get the vision clear. Rushing wastes work.
- Brand thinking starts with leadership and the product, not with marketing.
- A packaged offer ladder: a sprint, a foundation, a flagship. A discovery call comes first.

## What we take for May11Leven
- Every project: brief, then 4–5 concepts, then one idea, then merge, then present (the presentation is part of the work).
- Every project gets one line it rallies around, 8 words or fewer.
- Our offers get names and a ladder (to propose to Zayn; prices are his call).
- Copy test before anything ships: count words per line against the rules above.
