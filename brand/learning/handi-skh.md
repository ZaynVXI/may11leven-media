# HANDI identity, the skh. studio channel and a dark product-site showcase (three clips Zayn sent)

## HANDI: an identity that starts from a feeling
The designer's own words, in short: every identity project starts with questions. How do you make a service brand feel human? How do you show trust and reliability without the usual tools and trade symbols?

The answer began with a feeling, **"I've got you."** That feeling became the idea behind the symbol: support, reassurance, someone who has things handled.

**What we take (the method):**
1. **Ask the hard questions first.** What must this brand make people feel? What are the clichés of the category, and how do we avoid them (no hammer for a handyman, no tooth for a dentist, no house for real estate)?
2. **Name the feeling in a few plain words** ("I've got you", "nothing waits until morning", "you can still read it"). The feeling is the brief for the symbol, the tone of voice and the photography.
3. **Draw the feeling, not the trade.** The symbol expresses the feeling through gesture or form; it doesn't picture the service.
4. **Carry the feeling everywhere:**
   - verbal identity: "Anywhere. Anytime. We've got you."
   - a signature line: "Everyday services, thoughtfully handled"
   - photography of real people being helped
   - a friendly illustrated character
   - product animations
   - stationery, environment (office wall), app icon
5. **The caption pun closes the loop:** "let's handle it" for a brand called Handi. Our version: one line that plays on the client's own name or idea, never a generic CTA.

**How they presented it (format to adapt):**
- A **guideline scroll**: each page shows a tiny section index on the left (Logo & brand system, Our values, Creative direction, App icon, Colour palette, Photography, Product animations, Verbal identity, Signature language, Stationery, Environment, Tone of voice), with the current section highlighted and one big visual on the right.
- The index makes the system feel complete, and the scroll shows depth fast.
- It ends on the hero illustration and a card: "Full brand case study on Behance". Every reel points to the full case study.
- **Ours:** for each concept brand, publish the full case study (Behance or our site), and make the reel a guideline scroll with our own index style (Plex Mono, hairlines, the concept's colours). Never copy their layout or illustration style.

## skh. design studio (Shantanu Kumar, New Delhi): channel lessons
- **Who:** a brand identity designer and YouTuber with an engineering background; minimalist; teaches logo design (Skillshare) and writes on UX Planet. He spoke at Config APAC 2024 on representing culture through design.
- **His logo process:**
  1. Research: brief, client talk, personas, competitors.
  2. Mind map of keywords.
  3. Moodboard of 30–40 references, leaving out competitor logos so they don't steer the design.
  4. Sketch in **black and white first** ("good colour can hide a bad design choice").
  5. Digitise with basic shapes.
  6. Then colour and type.
  7. Present with all assets.
- **Principles:** minimal means only the essentials, not just fewer lines. A distinctive element (the FedEx arrow) is what makes a mark memorable.
- **Content format, "how to design a brand that doesn't suck", episode 48:** a numbered series of cinematic studio vlogs. Warm light, desk B-roll (notebooks, pens, laptop, a tin package, cutting paper by hand), the screen of references, one line of small centred type across every shot, ending on a big serif "EPISODE 48" title card.
- **Ours:**
  - A numbered studio series ("Studio notes 01, 02…") made of our own B-roll: Blender viewport, test renders, our desk objects shot in Blender, free stock hands, Destockd archive cutaways.
  - One small line of type, real music, and an episode card in our type.
  - No faces from his videos; Zayn stays off camera unless he chooses otherwise.

## Dark product-site showcase (a defence-tech style site)
- **What it shows:**
  - A dark, gritty world: phones and laptops on black metal and stone, with huge product names (Altius, Fury, Roadrunner) set in a tight grotesk.
  - One big numeral ("712 Open Roles").
  - Each product gets its own colour card (olive, sand, red, yellow) in a repeating grid.
  - Blue band hero sections and black-and-white factory photography.
- **What we take:**
  - For industrial, tech, mining, logistics or energy clients: a dark base with one colour per product line, huge type and real (or Destockd archive) photography.
  - The same "one colour per item" logic as our service colours.
  - Big numbers only when they're real.
