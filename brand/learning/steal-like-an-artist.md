# Steal Like an Artist (Austin Kleon): how we use it
Our working notes on the book's ideas (paraphrased, not quoted).
- Nothing is fully original; good work is a remix of what you collect. Keep a swipe file (brand/refs) and feed it every day.
- Good theft vs bad theft: honour, study, steal from many, credit, transform, remix. Bad: degrade, skim, steal from one, plagiarise, imitate, rip off.
- Copy your heroes to learn, then move past copying: steal their thinking (why the layout works), not the surface. Practice copies live in brand/practice and are never posted.
- Climb the family tree: find who your heroes learned from (studios Holographik credits, Swiss/International style, Dieter Rams-style restraint, modern product UI) and study them too.
- Don't wait to know who you are: make things, the style shows up through practice.
- Use your hands: sketch, build, render, record; not only research.
- Side projects matter: concepts for our markets are our side projects that become portfolio.
- Do good work and share it: show process, credit sources and collaborators.
- Be kind, stay consistent, keep a routine: daily study + daily practice, log progress.
- Creativity is subtraction: choose limits (one face, one accent, one hero) and remove until it breaks.
## Our rules from it
1. Every piece steals from at least 3 references, never one (state them in the brief).
2. Transform: change market, material, product and layout system so it is ours.
3. Practise by copying a reference layout 1:1 in private, then post only the transformed version.
4. Fidelity: any UI we show must look like a shipped product (real states, real data, icons, status bar, spacing system, imagery). Wireframe-looking screens never go out.
