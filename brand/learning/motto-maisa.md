# Motto × Maisa: how a perfect identity post is built

Maisa sells "Digital Workers" (AI that runs business processes) to banks and regulated industries. Founder's verdict: "the Instagram presentation, the mockup, the details, the design and everything… perfectly done."

## 1. The idea is trust, said plainly
- Hero line on the site: "Digital Workers that run entire processes, reliably." Under it: "Resistant to hallucinations, traceable, auditable."
- Strategy deck lines: "Real Work Ready", "AI you can trust", "High stakes demand the highest standards."
- Feature names are promises about failure: "Trace every action", "Model-agnostic", "Self-healing Workers".
**For us:** this is exactly the point the founder made about After hours. An AI brand sells reliability, not magic. Our agent posts lead with what is controlled, traced and recoverable (see may11leven-agent-guardrails).

## 2. One mark, many jobs
- The mark is seven three-pointed shapes in a ring. The same shapes, rearranged, become the feature illustrations on the site: a scattered cluster for "trace", an open ring for "model-agnostic", a tight core for "self-healing". The mark is the icon system.
- The wordmark and the mark are shown being built: outlines with anchor points, letter by letter, then the points collapse into the finished logo.
**For us:** pick primitives that can carry meaning when rearranged (Term's tick and name tape can do this; After hours needs its own).

## 3. Type and colour
- A serif display face for big statements, a clean grotesk for the interface. The serif is shown huge, with its curve points visible ("High stakes demand the highest standards.").
- Base: deep forest green, bone, near-black, with a soft periwinkle blue. An extended palette (coral, blue, mint, lime, pink) is shown once, as moving colour bands, and never spills into the core look.

## 4. The six images: how each is presented
1. **Laptop in a warm room, cropped.** The laptop runs off the right edge; the site's feature row reads big; a warm wall and a wooden table edge set the mood. The device isn't centred or whole.
2. **Interface close-up on a sky-blue gradient.** One panel, cropped hard: the mark, "Create New", a list with one item selected and a cursor. It reads like a real product moment.
3. **Device collage on white.** Phones and tablets in thick black frames, all cropped by the edges, showing the splash, hero, case studies, a mobile section and an event screen. It's dense but calm, because everything aligns.
4. **Merch: the mark embroidered on the back of a green polo.** No face. Shot close, so you see the stitches and the fabric weave.
5. **A film still with a glassy interface pill on top.** "Watch 15:64 · Chris Reeding — UBS Bank" sits over a calm, grainy photo. Product and life in one frame.
6. **A business card in a steel case on green linen, with a black leather strap.** Every material is real, and the green of the brand is carried by the surface.

**Patterns:**
- **Crop, don't centre.** Devices bleed off the edge. Close-ups beat full views.
- **Real materials carry the colour:** linen, steel, leather, pique cotton, a plaster wall.
- **No pure-text slides in the carousel.** The words live inside the design (the site, the deck, the pill) and in the caption.
- **Variety in one world:** device, UI close-up, collage, merch, lifestyle, stationery. Six presentation types, one colour world.

## 5. The five reels (6–19 s)
1. **Logo construction:** letters as outlines with anchor points, building up, then the mark, the wordmark, and the website scrolling inside device frames.
2. **The strategy deck:** dozens of pages tiled into a grid that slowly zooms out, with "Real Work Ready" and "AI you can trust" surfacing. It proves the depth of thinking without a single word of caption.
3. **Palette:** colour bands sliding and stacking.
4. **Type:** giant serif letters with curve points, resolving into "High stakes demand the highest standards."
5. **Agent Hawkeye:** the brand checked by Motto's own QA agent (proportions, type, grid), ending on "Identity Validated".
Only one has sound. The rest are silent loops built to be watched twice.

## What we change because of this
1. **Cropped devices and close-ups** become the default. Term v3's slide 4 shows the whole phone centred; next time, crop.
2. **At most one text slide per carousel**, and only when it's a designed object. Term v3 has three text-led slides (2, 6, 8). The next round turns the "what if" content into a designed object (a form, a ledger, a stamp sheet), not paragraphs.
3. **Merch and material shots** for every product world. For Term: a real name tape sewn into the collar of the maroon jumper, shot close like Maisa's embroidery.
4. **A process reel per product:** construction (points to mark), system (deck grid zoom-out), or QA (our preflight shown step by step).
5. **Overlay pills on real photographs** to put the product inside life (After hours: a guest's chat pill over a dusk rooftop photo).
6. **Trust language for every AI product:** traceable, recoverable, a person in the loop. Said plainly.
