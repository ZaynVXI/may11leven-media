# Logo and identity study: Aperios Design (Elio, Nomint), the Brand Identity interview, Logo Archive

## Alex Aperios: "Too many brands play it safe" (the-brandidentity.com interview)
- Playing safe is the risk. Brands that copy their category look (often minimal black, white and grey) blend in and get forgotten. Attention is harder to earn now, so push further. Not every client will want that.
- Look inward, not at competitors. A competitor only tells you what has already been done; the client's own story gives the reason to stand apart.
- Creative block comes from designing before understanding the purpose and audience. Ask more questions, research deeper and collaborate, and you guess less and revise less.
- Where the brief allows: hand-drawn lettering, added texture and layered identities feel more human. Lettering needs more sketching time.
- Read the case study before judging someone else's rebrand.
- (He uses Midjourney for backgrounds and mockups. We don't: no AI imagery is our rule.)

## Elio (mortgage brand), what to steal
- Story: buying a home is exciting, but the process is stressful. Brand idea: make it warm and reassuring, like sunrise.
- The mark is a sunburst of tapered rays next to a friendly lowercase sans wordmark. The rays become the whole system: giant crops bleeding off the page, outline versions, glow versions, and shapes that cut photos.
- Colour: oxblood, vermilion, orange, periwinkle blue and limestone. Gradients come from the sun (sky blue into orange into red) and are used as media, not on buttons.
- Type: a condensed serif for headlines and a plain sans for body.
- Info graphics: line drawings (stacked diamonds with an up arrow, concentric circles with dots, cylinders as bar charts) make the numbers easier to grasp.
- Stickers: circular rotating text badges ("Built for every borrower") with an arrow.
- Presentation: rounded cards stacked on black, scrolled slowly in the reel, with hex swatches named ("Limestone").
- The caption worked (about 1.4k likes, 392 shares): a strong identity starts with a story, then the problem, the idea, how each choice served the idea, the lesson, the case study link and a DM CTA.

## Nomint (animation studio)
- A custom pixel-stepped wordmark set on a slant, with a hidden face: the "oo" are eyes and a stepped smile sits under them. It's playful but still one bold black shape.
- One loud colour (mint green), black and white. Dark green for the business card scene.
- Applications: letterhead with a giant wordmark, a vertical wordmark on a mint card, a business card with a blind emboss plus the face as a standalone icon, a tee shot in a real cactus garden, a poster on a leather sofa, and a tablet website in real light.
- Lesson: the logo has a personality you can extract (the face works as an icon on its own).

## Aperios grid patterns
- Character marks, lettering, monograms (RS, AA, WF), and marks embossed on paper or carved into stone and rendered.
- Logos on real-world objects: a bag, a building sign, a tee, a poster.
- Mostly one mark per tile with a strong colour field. Varied, but always bold.

## Logo Archive (logo-archive.org) and the logo-gallery reel
- Logo Archive (Richard Baird, BP&O) has 5,500+ researched historical, mostly modernist logos, tagged by technique, with new ones added daily. It's a paid subscription, so we don't subscribe (no paid tools). The free parts are their Substack notes and Instagram posts, plus BP&O articles.
- The gallery reel shows how marks are catalogued by form-making technique. That list is our logo toolbox: single letter, two letters/monogram, negative space, asymmetrical, symmetrical, solid, outline, lines, circle, square, dynamic, open, closed, figurative, animals, numbers, wordmark.
- Lesson from the modernists: a logo is the most flexible asset (app icon to signage), drawn with care, and built from a toolbox of techniques, not a shortcut.

## How this changes our work (still minimal)
- Minimal is not "safe and grey". Our minimal means few elements, one strong idea and real craft. The idea, colour and character can be bold. Every client gets their own world, never the category default.
- Every logo starts with story and questions, then 3+ technique explorations from the toolbox, then one mark that extends into a system (crops, patterns, stickers, icon, motion, photo cut-outs).
- Present the identity on real objects in real light: Blender renders or free stock scenes, never AI.
- Rebrand and case-study captions follow the Elio pattern: story, problem, idea, choices, lesson, CTA.
