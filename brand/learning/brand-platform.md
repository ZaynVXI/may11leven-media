# Brand strategy platform (from Motto, plus free frameworks)

## Motto (wearemotto.com): how they work
- Belief: the culture is the brand and the brand is the culture. They align leadership, culture and brand around one central idea, which they trademark as the "Idea Worth Rallying Around". We don't use their trademarked terms; ours is "the one idea".
- The method keeps vision, culture and brand from drifting apart as a company grows: idea, then vision, then culture (behaviours, values, norms), then brand (product, experience, communication).
- Phases (Design Wanted interview):
  - immersion (leadership and culture)
  - brand strategy
  - verbal identity
  - visual identity (style and mood boards for colour, type, imagery and texture, then a few identity pathways, each prototyped on key applications)
- Research informs but doesn't dictate. They sometimes go against the data. "You can't build an effective brand without a strong point of view."
- The one idea must be innovative, impactful, sustainable, aligned with real strengths and aspirational. Steps: discover (free brainstorm plus market and trends), iterate (unmet needs, SWOT, feedback), validate (prototypes, tests, customer interviews).
  - Examples: Goodnotes "Notes Reimagined", FasterLines "Why Wait", Bandana "Jobs for the People", Maisa "Real Work Ready".

## Maisa (AI digital workers, USD 25M Series A), the case Zayn sent
- Problem: around 95% of enterprise gen-AI pilots fail (MIT NANDA, via TechCrunch). Enterprises can't trust black-box AI.
- Maisa's edge: transparency, traceability and accountability. You see and approve the steps before the AI acts ("chain-of-work").
- Idea: "Real Work Ready". The brand makes autonomous AI feel tangible and trustworthy.
- Signature move: a custom generative tool makes a unique animated icon for every digital worker, grown from the core symbol, so the identity evolves as the company scales.
- Scope list (a model for our proposals):
  - immersion and research
  - leadership workshops
  - strategy and positioning
  - the one idea
  - messaging and verbal identity
  - visual identity and design system
  - motion identity
  - custom generative tool
  - icon library
  - guidelines

- A flag mark (a rally flag, literally the idea), black and white.
- Workbook series: dark covers with a hairline grid, one neon colour per book (coral, green, blue) and a big number (01/02/03). It's a colour-coded system: minimal, but instantly a set.
- The Future of Brand Report 2026 page:
  - black page, a dot-matrix display type that animates and scrambles
  - plain grotesk statements
  - numbered "forces" with small image chips: brand as operating system, alignment is the new speed, clarity through subtraction, clone wars demand distinction, synergy of human and machine
- The grid mixes system diagrams (the mark on a construction grid), devices with real UI, dark reports and moody photography.

- Content mix: yearly work reels (2020-2025, short and fast, 24 seconds in 2024), a manifesto film, case films (Ninjas in Pyjamas, "Expect the Unexpected": a strategy for internal unity plus a new identity), the founders' book (Rare Breed), a speaker reel, and Bloomberg on branding in the age of AI.
- Manifesto copy pattern: a short line per beat. "Companies are more than names. They're ideas. Big ideas transform culture... We've been doing it for 19 years... Why not hire a team whose ambition is as big as yours?"
- The "Idea People" AI film: they treat AI as an extension of the director's vision, say no to most outputs and refine toward one aesthetic. Note for us: we don't publish AI imagery, but the principle (taste and the discipline to say no) applies to every tool we use.
- Working with leaders: they interview clients as much as clients interview them, look for leaders willing to change, watch how the company behaves when no one is looking, walk away from poor fits, educate clients on brand terms, and put substance over surface.
- Questions they ask: Why does the business exist? Why do you come to work each day? What are you trying to change in the world? What's changing for you, and what future do you see?

## Steal Like an Artist: what we take from Motto, and how we transform it
Steal the thinking, never the look or their trademarks. Mix them with our other influences (Holographik, Aperios, .raw, Lebedev) so the result is ours.
1. **One idea per client**, crossed with problem-first. Our version always names the African-market problem it solves (e.g. Inzu: "sell the mornings, not the room count").
2. **Series systems**, from their colour-coded workbooks. Transformed: an "11" series, one layout with one accent per edition and a big index number. It's already our 11.01 tag device, now applied to whole series.
3. **A studio report**, from the Future of Brand Report. Transformed: "11 Field Notes", a short problem-first report on one target market each month (agri-trade, diaspora food, the EU accessibility law, Kigali hospitality). Black page, one display-type device, numbered findings with sources, and lead capture by "comment 11".
4. **Generative identity**, from the Maisa icons. Transformed: we code a small tool that grows assets from a client's mark (an icon per branch, product or agent) as a premium add-on. No AI imagery: it's code-made.
5. **Manifesto film**, from the Motto films. Transformed: a 20-second minimal reel with kinetic type, real renders and our core markets ("Built for the brands Africa will export next").
6. **Client fit**, from "rare breed" leaders. Transformed: a short fit questionnaire before free audits (why do you exist, what's changing, will you act on the notes?).

## Motto's style: type, colour and why
### Type
- Motto Supply's site uses **PP Neue Montreal** (Pangram Pangram), found in the store's own code. The Motto site itself is behind a bot check, so we didn't look further. Their decks, reports and posts use the same family of look: a neutral neo-grotesk, set big and tight in uppercase for headlines ("WHAT'S ON OUR SHELF", "FUTURE OF BRAND REPORT 2026") and sentence case for statements.
- Why a neo-grotesk: it reads as neutral, confident and "operating system" rather than decorative. It lets the idea and the work be the hero, and it works at every size from a billboard to UI.
- A second voice, only when it carries meaning: a **dot-matrix display type** for the AI/future report (machine and signal), and serif or soft custom letterforms inside client brands where warmth is the point (ivo).
- The logotype has custom ink traps. Small craft details make a plain grotesk feel owned.
- Numbering as structure: 001, 002 lists; 01/02/03 workbooks; "(1)". Numbers carry the sequence. This is the same device as our 11.01 tag.
- PP Neue Montreal is paid for commercial use. Our free equivalents are Inter Tight (already ours), Host Grotesk, Instrument Sans and Hanken Grotesk. Use them the same way: one grotesk, big and tight, with hierarchy from size and grey.

### Colour
- Base: almost black and white, now cool grey and white ("more approachable" than all-black, per the-brandidentity interview). It's monochrome because the Brutalist, honest, direct voice wants nothing decorative.
- Accent: one saturated colour per item or category, used to tell content apart. Coral, neon green and electric blue workbooks; Klein blue planner; mint on deep green; orange and magenta on the Rare Breed book. Colour is a filing system, not decoration.
- Client work: each client gets its own world. Cresta is electric blue with chrome and silver 3D ribbons and device UI; ivo is deep moss green with misty landscapes; Maisa is black with one acid-green dot.
- Secondary text is mid grey for hierarchy (we keep our contrast floor).

### Layout and presentation
- Big white or grey canvas, generous margins, product shots on light grey tiles with soft shadows, centred objects, and a tiny brand mark in a corner.
- A store page scroll: a centred product column on grey, with the logo left and the item name right. It's calm, gallery-like and slow.
- Article and report pages on phones: a big image, a grotesk headline and short paragraphs. They show editorial thinking, not just visuals.
- Guidelines shown as clean web pages: sections in a list (logo, typography, colour, photography, motion, signature language), with each one opening into a full-bleed visual.
- Taste curation as content: "What's on our shelf, edition 001" lists the books, tools and podcasts they use (Pith planner, Cosmos, New Utilitarian, Acquired, Rare Breed, Jitter, Browsing Mode, Patch Work by Logo Archive). It builds authority without selling.

### Steal like an artist (our versions)
- **"11 on the shelf, edition 001":** a carousel of the free tools, books and sites we actually learn from (Steal Like an Artist, Logo Archive's free notes, Refero, Poly Haven, Blender, the EAA guide). Numbered 11.01-11.08, white page, one grotesk, product shots on grey tiles (Blender-rendered books or free stock). It shows taste and builds trust. Credit every source.
- **Series colour coding:** each service gets one accent in our posts (brand, web, apps, packaging, AI automation), on the same grid and layout. It reads like Motto's workbooks.
- **Report pages:** the 11 Field Notes as a dark page with one display device. For us that's a dot or tick device from our ring kit, not a copy of their dot-matrix.
