# How studios actually get clients

## The evidence
- **Referrals win, by far.** SparkToro's State of Digital Agencies 2025 puts referrals from current and past clients "far and away" first. 15% name partner companies as their top referral source. Speaking at events rose to 4th. RSW/US 2025 (professional services firms in the US and Canada) found the same top three: referrals, networking, speaking.
- **Social media is a weak direct source**, but where it works, **LinkedIn is the channel** (73% of agencies that use social rate it effective).
- **Outbound is mostly mediocre:** 59% tried it, and only 9% call it very effective.
- **Most agencies have no dedicated marketer (79%) or salesperson (70%)**, and only 14% call their pipeline healthy. A studio with a system stands out.

## What Motto does (from their own FAQ and blog)
- **A narrow ideal client:** Series C/D, pre-IPO and newly public tech companies, global enterprises. They sell to leaders **at a moment of change** (funding, IPO, transformation).
- **Named, fixed packages** (Foundation / Flagship / Fullsail, plus FastTrack for funded startups), so buyers know what they're getting.
- **A fit conversation before any proposal.** They answer an RFP only if they can meet the CEO or founders, so they never pitch blind.
- **Terms:** 50% upfront, the rest monthly; 6–8 months for a full brand, 16+ weeks for FastTrack.
- **Proof everywhere:** dozens of Clutch reviews, awards, a bestselling book and media columns. Buyers arrive already trusting them.

## .raw
Co-founded a festival (Dragon Days), runs monthly events and a café/store. **Clients meet them in person before they need them.** Listed on Sortlist and DesignRush.

## The thinking behind it (Blair Enns, David C. Baker)
- **Specialise.** Expertise in one field makes referrals and inbound easier ("they're the hotel people").
- **Diagnose before you prescribe.** Lead with a diagnosis, not free design. Our "free first look" is a diagnosis, never spec work.
- **Don't pitch for free against other studios.** Sell the thinking.

## Our plan (fits our rules: we never contact leads ourselves; Zayn decides any outreach)
1. **Be specialist-first:** hospitality, real estate and interiors in East Africa and the Gulf. One niche at a time, starting with small hotels and guesthouses (research + calculator + Lamu flagship).
2. **First clients → referrals.** One or two pilot clients (Zayn's call), done brilliantly, with honest results and permission to tell the story. That's where referrals start.
3. **Partners (the 15%)**, for Zayn to approach if he chooses: booking-engine and payment providers (e.g. Pesapal), photographers (Steez Visualz's network), web developers who don't do brand, startup hubs and accelerators in Kigali and Nairobi, tour operators.
4. **LinkedIn as the main business channel**, posting only work that passes the Motto test, with original research as the lead format.
5. **Proof assets:** working free tools and published research now, real reviews later (Clutch, Google). We never write reviews ourselves.
6. **Free directory listings** (Clutch, Sortlist, DesignRush, Google Business Profile). Zayn creates the accounts; we write the profiles.
7. **Named packages with "from" prices:** Mark (from $800); Storefront and After hours waiting on Zayn's prices. Each starts with a short paid or free diagnosis.
8. **Trigger moments:** watch funding rounds, hotel openings, new branches and expansions to the Gulf, and post useful takes on them. Zayn reaches out personally if he wants to.
9. **Later:** a talk or workshop (Zayn's choice, since the page itself never shows him) and an event or meet-up in Kigali once he's there.

## Nonstop (Manchester), studied from nonstopbrandingagency.com
The studio behind the Bloom brand in the Figma Motion video. The founder's YouTube and Instagram videos (talking head, brand assets on screen) feed the agency.
- **Niche plus a promise in the first line:** "We build brands that get SaaS, AI and fintech companies noticed in 4-8 weeks using our 4-step DIVE process." Who, result, time, named method.
- **Named process:** DIVE (4 steps). A name makes the method feel owned and repeatable.
- **Case-study cards:** client, category, year, then a 3–6 word line ("Own the Hallway", "A companion in your pocket"). Numbered (1/7).
- **Prices in public, as a range:** guidelines from about £4k, a full identity closer to £20k. It filters out bad-fit leads before the call.
- **Low-risk first step:** "Book a free brand audit." Same job as our "Comment 11, free first look".
- **FAQ answers the doubts:** time, cost, outside the UK, what you walk away with, ongoing design, how to start, who you work with, "we already have a logo". 4–9 words per question.
- **Trust lines:** the person who pitches is the person who designs; no handoffs to juniors.
- **Recurring income:** a monthly design retainer after launch.
- **Second product:** Designer HQ, an education site for designers, sold alongside the studio.
- **Page is short:** hero, 7 projects, 8 FAQs, footer. Nothing else.

### What we take
1. Our first line needs the same four parts: who, result, time, named method. Draft: "May11Leven Labs builds brands, websites and apps for hotels and growing businesses in East Africa, in 4–6 weeks." Time and method name to be agreed with Zayn.
2. Name our process. Candidates to propose: ELEVEN-style steps are tempting but forced; better a plain 4-step name from our real method (Research, Idea, Build, Proof).
3. Publish "from" prices once Zayn sets them.
4. Our site FAQ uses the same doubts as the Second Thoughts series, so the posts and the site sell the same answers.
5. Founder-style video is how Nonstop markets; we can't show a face, so our equivalent is screen-and-hands process videos with no face.

## Update: marketing strategy around our services (second research round)
**What changed in the evidence**
- **Word of mouth is weakening for everyone.** RSW/US's 2026 outlook: the share of marketers finding agencies through networking fell from 73% to 58% (2022 to 2025), through past relationships from 67% to 48%, and through friends or co-workers from 60% to 35%. Marketers now find agencies through search, AI platforms, content and direct outreach, and want concrete outcomes, not vague AI promises (Agility PR, RSW/US 2026). For a new studio with no past clients, that's good news: being findable and visibly useful matters more than a network we don't have yet.
- **Instagram rewards sends and originality.** Mosseri names watch time, likes and sends as the top signals; sends (DMs) matter most for reaching non-followers, and original creators get more distribution than reposts. Buffer's analysis of 4M+ posts: carousels earn 12% more engagement than reels, reels get 36% more reach (Buffer, 2026).

**Our strategy from here**
1. **Make every "free first look" real and specific to a service.** Not "comment 11 for a free look" alone, but a named, useful thing per service: an accessibility first-look report for a web shop (Ally, see brand/ideas/idea-scan-2.md), a brand clinic at 16 px, a homepage review, a photo-consent check (Nod). A real deliverable is the proof a new studio lacks.
2. **Post things people send to their boss.** Law-and-money explainers travel by DM: "Is your shop accessible under the EU law? 5 checks", "Can you post this guest's photo?", "Which of your expenses will KRA reject?". One sendable explainer, then the product carousel, then the reel. Carousels for engagement, reels for reach.
3. **Be findable by search and AI.** Our own site needs real case pages with clear questions and answers, structured so AI search can cite them (ai-seo skill). Clients now shortlist before they ever message us.
4. **Partners instead of a network.** Social media managers in Nairobi (Nod as the consent layer inside their retainer), accountants (Receipt), web agencies without accessibility skills (Ally, white-label). Partners were 15% of agencies' top referral source in SparkToro's data.
5. **LinkedIn for the B2B ideas.** Accessibility (EU) and compliance (Kenya) are LinkedIn topics; keep posts professional, sourced, and in documents people save.
6. **Credit and case rights in every project.** Each client project ends with a case study we're allowed to publish, so referrals have something to point at.
