# How studios actually get clients

## The evidence
- **Referrals win, by far.** SparkToro's State of Digital Agencies 2025 puts referrals from current and past clients "far and away" first. 15% name partner companies as their top referral source. Speaking at events rose to 4th. RSW/US 2025 (professional services firms in the US and Canada) found the same top three: referrals, networking, speaking.
- **Social media is a weak direct source**, but where it works, **LinkedIn is the channel** (73% of agencies that use social rate it effective).
- **Outbound is mostly mediocre:** 59% tried it, and only 9% call it very effective.
- **Most agencies have no dedicated marketer (79%) or salesperson (70%)**, and only 14% call their pipeline healthy. A studio with a system stands out.

## What Motto does (from their own FAQ and blog)
- **A narrow ideal client:** Series C/D, pre-IPO and newly public tech companies, global enterprises. They sell to leaders **at a moment of change** (funding, IPO, transformation).
- **Named, fixed packages** (Foundation / Flagship / Fullsail, plus FastTrack for funded startups), so buyers know what they're getting.
- **A fit conversation before any proposal.** They answer an RFP only if they can meet the CEO or founders, so they never pitch blind.
- **Terms:** 50% upfront, the rest monthly; 6–8 months for a full brand, 16+ weeks for FastTrack.
- **Proof everywhere:** dozens of Clutch reviews, awards, a bestselling book and media columns. Buyers arrive already trusting them.

## .raw
Co-founded a festival (Dragon Days), runs monthly events and a café/store. **Clients meet them in person before they need them.** Listed on Sortlist and DesignRush.

## The thinking behind it (Blair Enns, David C. Baker)
- **Specialise.** Expertise in one field makes referrals and inbound easier ("they're the hotel people").
- **Diagnose before you prescribe.** Lead with a diagnosis, not free design. Our "free first look" is a diagnosis, never spec work.
- **Don't pitch for free against other studios.** Sell the thinking.

## Our plan (fits our rules: we never contact leads ourselves; Zayn decides any outreach)
1. **Be specialist-first:** hospitality, real estate and interiors in East Africa and the Gulf. One niche at a time, starting with small hotels and guesthouses (research + calculator + Lamu flagship).
2. **First clients → referrals.** One or two pilot clients (Zayn's call), done brilliantly, with honest results and permission to tell the story. That's where referrals start.
3. **Partners (the 15%)**, for Zayn to approach if he chooses: booking-engine and payment providers (e.g. Pesapal), photographers (Steez Visualz's network), web developers who don't do brand, startup hubs and accelerators in Kigali and Nairobi, tour operators.
4. **LinkedIn as the main business channel**, posting only work that passes the Motto test, with original research as the lead format.
5. **Proof assets:** working free tools and published research now, real reviews later (Clutch, Google). We never write reviews ourselves.
6. **Free directory listings** (Clutch, Sortlist, DesignRush, Google Business Profile). Zayn creates the accounts; we write the profiles.
7. **Named packages with "from" prices:** Mark (from $800); Storefront and Night shift waiting on Zayn's prices. Each starts with a short paid or free diagnosis.
8. **Trigger moments:** watch funding rounds, hotel openings, new branches and expansions to the Gulf, and post useful takes on them. Zayn reaches out personally if he wants to.
9. **Later:** a talk or workshop (Zayn's choice, since the page itself never shows him) and an event or meet-up in Kigali once he's there.
