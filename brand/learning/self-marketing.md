# How top studios market themselves when they're not posting projects

## What they actually do (researched)
| Studio | Move | What it does for them |
|---|---|---|
| **Motto** | A bestselling book (*Rare Breed*), founders as known voices (Fast Company columns, Thinkers50, podcasts), registered thinking tools ("Idea Worth Rallying Around®"), opinion reels about "future branding" | Sells their **way of thinking**, so clients buy the method, not just the pictures |
| **.raw** | A café and concept store (.store) at Slovenska 58, a monthly event (.hangout), co-founded a deep-tech festival (Dragon Days, 500+ people) | The studio becomes a **place and a community**; clients come to them |
| **Koto** | Newsletter "Off Brand": one topic in depth, practical frameworks given away free; 2,500 → 6,000 subscribers | **Educates buyers before they're ready to hire**; positions the studio "as a media company" |
| **Order** | Standards Manual (publishing) and Standards (a brand-guidelines tool): 10,000 sign-ups on day one against a 1,000 target | **Products built from their expertise** reach people who'd never see a case study |
| **Collins** | Essays and interviews ("Reflections"), awards, culture as a selling point | Credibility and recruiting |
| **Pentagram** | Partners' names and interviews in the design press | Reputation through people and **earned media** |
| **Locomotive** | Open-source code (Locomotive Scroll) used by developers worldwide | Technical credibility that markets itself |
| **Holographik** | "Site of the Day" tiles set in each project's own type | **Real awards as content** |
| **Brandon** (Design Week) | A weekly digest built from what the team already shares, reposted to LinkedIn and Instagram | Low-effort and consistent; clients mention it in meetings |

**The pattern:** the best studios sell **a point of view, useful things and a world around the studio**. Case studies are only one part.

## Our constraints (they shape the strategy)
- No founder face or name: the **studio** is the voice. That fits Koto's and Order's model, not Pentagram's.
- Never reveal how the studio runs. No fake clients, testimonials, results or physical spaces.
- Free tools only. We never contact leads; people come to us.

## Our version: six content pillars, each tied to services
1. **Point of view (Motto).** Sharp, researched opinions on business in our markets, each one a short essay, an opinion reel or a LinkedIn text post. Examples: "In Nairobi the phone is the shop." "Your Booking.com listing is your real homepage." "A logo is judged at 16 px before it's judged at 16 m." Each opinion is backed by a number we can source and ends with the service it relates to.
2. **Free tools (Order, Locomotive).** Small, working, useful tools, each one a demo of a service: Check (AI agents), Commission calculator (web + automation), a 16 px logo test that runs on your own file (brand), a WhatsApp chat-flow sketcher (automation), and later a palette-from-a-photo tool (brand). Tools get shared, saved and linked. They're also our honest proof.
3. **Original research (the extraordinary one).** "We tested the websites of 30 Zanzibar guesthouses: load time on a mid-range phone, whether you can book without leaving the site, M-Pesa or card, WhatsApp reply time." All public data, published as a designed report plus a carousel and a LinkedIn document. Nobody in our markets does this. It's press-worthy, and it makes us the expert in a niche before we have clients there. Repeat per niche: Kigali restaurants, Nairobi clinics, Dubai real-estate agents.
4. **Field Notes (Koto).** A monthly long read: one topic in depth, practical and free (e.g. "Branding a guesthouse on a budget: what to spend on first"). Cut into a carousel, a LinkedIn document and a story. Later an email list, when Zayn decides.
5. **How we think (Motto's method).** Name and show our own process: Problem → One line → System → Proof. Show tests, rejected directions, measurements and why we cut things. Real process from our concept and tool work, never staged.
6. **Studio world (.raw).** About 1 in 6 posts: our type, mark, colours, merch designed and rendered in Blender (labelled as design, not as things that exist until they do), the tools on a desk, and a studio manifesto. No fake office.

Projects and case studies (Flagship worlds) are then about 1 in 3 posts, not every post.

## Every post still passes the Motto test (post-budget.md)
And every pillar names the service it sells, once and late, with one ask.
