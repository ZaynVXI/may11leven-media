# Learn UI Design: short, tactical, proven

## Method (how he teaches)
- **Tactics, not theory.** He skips colour wheels, golden ratio and grid theory. A rule earns its place only if it makes a design better today.
- **Numbered, named rules.** "7 rules", "the 4 most important rules", "6 strategies". Each rule has a short imperative name you can remember.
- **Before and after on every rule.** Annotated screenshots with numbered callouts, magnified details and a caption.
- **Watch over a pro's shoulder.** Blank canvas to finished design, live. Process sells.
- **Steal like an artist.** Copy the best until you're good, then find your own style. (Same as our rule.)

## The 7 rules, one line each
1. Light comes from the sky: tops lighter, bottoms darker. Inset fields, outset buttons.
2. Black and white first. Add one or two colours later, each with a job.
3. Double your whitespace.
4. Text on images: a 35% black overlay, a scrim, a floor fade, a blur or a dark box. Test at every size.
5. Make text pop and un-pop with size, colour, weight, case and spacing. Only the page title is fully loud. Hover states change colour or background, never size or weight.
6. Use only good fonts: clean neutrals for UI, character fonts only for strong brands. Skip the lightest weights.
7. Steal like an artist.

## Copy (why it feels short)
- Headlines are one promise: "Confidently design beautiful user interfaces for any app or site."
- Benefits are three short lines, verb first.
- One-line paragraphs for emphasis. Fragments are fine: "Put space between your lines."
- Lessons are 3–4 bullets. Newsletter promise: "5-minute lessons".
- One CTA, repeated: "Apply Now".

## Marketing (how he gets students)
- **Free tools:** Accessible Color Generator, Data Viz Color Picker, Gradient and Mesh Gradient generators, Interactive Typography Tutorial. Useful on their own, they rank in search and lead to the course.
- **Evergreen articles** (7 Rules) that people share for years.
- **Newsletter** with short lessons (60k+ readers).
- **Proof:** named testimonials with job titles, a student count, a refund guarantee, and a "not right for you if…" list.

## Our rules from him
1. Every line we write gets cut in half, then checked again. One idea per line.
2. Every educational post is a named rule with a before and after we actually designed.
3. Build free tools that work on their own (the commission calculator first) and link them from every report.
4. Add a "not right for you if…" line to our service pages. It builds trust.
5. Only one thing on a page is fully loud.
