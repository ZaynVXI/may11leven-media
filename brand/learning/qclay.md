# Qclay: what to learn (studied from 16 posts Zayn sent)

qclay.design is blocked from our workspace, so this note works from the saved posts in `brand/refs/qclay/01–16.jpg`. Steal the thinking, never the surface.

## What they are
A product design studio (apps, websites, brands) that sells itself mostly through **product concepts**: digital products that don't exist yet, designed as if they shipped. Every post ends with the same offer: "We are open for new projects. We design → Mobile apps → Websites → Brands."

## What they build (the concept types)
- **Money app (Aura):** frosted cards for Reserve, Spend and Grow; exact euro figures; a small mono "ON TRACK" pill.
- **Developer tool (Kiln, GPU profiler):** a treemap headed "Every training step, tile by tile.", a heatmap, a utilisation menu.
- **AI agent (Nightshift, on-call):** "Incidents get fixed while your team is still asleep." A terminal log and an incidents dashboard over pixelated night photography.
- **Utility app (speed test):** one huge number (912.3 Mbps), glass widgets, a phone held in a hand.

## Why it works (the deep part)
1. **The product is the portfolio.** A concept shows a buyer exactly what they would get: screens, a system, a promise in one line. It proves app and AI skill without a client.
2. **One promise per product, written like a headline a founder would buy.** "Incidents get fixed while your team is still asleep." It names an outcome, not a feature.
3. **Hyper-specific data makes fiction feel shipped.** INC-3318 closed in 6 min, 912.3 Mbps, €12,480. Round numbers look fake; odd ones look measured.
4. **One hero metric per frame.** Everything else is small and grey.
5. **Fragments, not full screens.** Cards and panels float and crop at the edge, so the viewer imagines a bigger product.
6. **Texture behind glass.** UI over grain, dither or pixelated photos. The background gives mood; the UI stays crisp. That contrast is the "expensive" feeling.
7. **Mono labels as the only decoration.** Status pills, IDs and timestamps in a monospace face do the job other studios give to icons and gradients.
8. **Category fluency.** Each concept uses its category's real vocabulary (on-call, p95, utilisation, reserve). A CTO or CFO reads it and trusts it.
9. **Captions are an offer, not a story:** one line about the idea, then the list of services, then "open for new projects".

## What we take (transformed, not copied)
- **Promise-first headlines** for every working demo: one outcome a business owner would pay for, 8 words or fewer.
- **Measured data in every UI:** odd, believable numbers in local currencies (KES, AED, EUR), never round.
- **One hero number per slide**, set large in our type; everything else in Plex Mono at small size.
- **Fragment framing** for app and AI posts: 2–3 cropped panels on a real material or our own grain, not a full phone every time.
- **Texture behind crisp UI:** our own photographs (window light, stone, paper) blurred or dithered, never their pixel-night look.
- **Our own categories**, so nothing reads as theirs: hotel booking, restaurant ordering, school fees, logistics, clinic queues. These match the industries we sell to.

## What we never take
- Their names (Aura, Kiln, Nightshift), layouts, frosted-glass card style as a whole, their exact caption line, or their pixel-photo backgrounds.
- **Naming clash:** our automation package called "Night shift" is too close to their Nightshift concept. Rename it (options: "After hours", "Closing time", "Overnight").
