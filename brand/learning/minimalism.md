# Minimalism principles

One principle per study session. Each one is a rule we can check on a frame, not a mood.

1. **Let the structure be the decoration.** Motto's workbook covers have no image at all: a hairline grid, a title, one big numeral. When a frame feels empty, add a visible system (grid, rule, numbered cell) before adding a picture. If the grid can't carry it, the idea isn't clear yet.
   - Check: could this frame be printed in one colour and still look finished?
   - Ours: Bone ground, ink hairline grid, the 11 mark in one cell, one service accent on the numeral only.

2. **One weight carries the whole system.** Estudio Repúblicas built RN Arquitectura on a single weight of Basel Classic, one olive hero colour and no symbol at all; Courier Prime appears only in technical documents (the-brandidentity.com). Hierarchy comes from size, position and space, never from bold.
   - Check: switch every weight on the frame to Regular. If the order of reading changes, the layout is leaning on weight instead of scale and space.
   - Ours: the Zanzibar 9.6 s tile uses Inter Display Regular only, from the 400 px numeral to 20 px captions. The one emphasis in the footer ("86%") is colour, not weight.

3. **The quiet object is noticed last.** Minimalissimo's reading of Jasper Morrison: the best objects don't announce themselves; "the balance of how they look and how they perform is correct" (buttondown.com/minimalissimo). The Kehikko chair is named for "framework" and keeps only what the form needs; iA's notebook is all-white with the detail in the guidelines, not the surface. Restraint is a performance test, not a look: strip until removing one more thing would break the job.
   - Check: name the one job of the screen in a sentence. If an element doesn't serve that sentence, it's decoration - cut it.
   - Ours: Mita's one job is "show what the last token actually bought". The hero numeral, the token string and the three-part split serve it; everything else (gradients, cards-within-cards, a second accent) was cut. The green block earns its place because it IS the reading, not a panel around one.

4. **When the subject is loud, the frame goes silent.** Motto's shelf shot of *Rare Breed* (refs/motto/shelf-07.jpg): an orange and magenta cover full of scribble, laid flat in the centre of a plain grey ground with nearly half the frame left empty. No label, no shadow play, no props. The cover does all the shouting, so the frame does none. Aperios does the same in reverse: a plain white tee hung against a wall of cactus (refs/aperios/aperios-07.jpg), so the quiet object stands out against a busy world.
   - Check: count the colours in the subject. If it has more than one, the ground gets none: Bone or Ink only, no labels, no accent.
   - Ours: when we show a client's real packaging in its own loud colours, it goes flat on Bone with a wide margin and nothing else. Our labels and the service colour move to the next slide.
