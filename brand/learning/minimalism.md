# Minimalism principles

One principle per study session. Each one is a rule we can check on a frame, not a mood.

1. **Let the structure be the decoration.** Motto's workbook covers have no image at all: a hairline grid, a title, one big numeral. When a frame feels empty, add a visible system (grid, rule, numbered cell) before adding a picture. If the grid can't carry it, the idea isn't clear yet.
   - Check: could this frame be printed in one colour and still look finished?
   - Ours: Bone ground, ink hairline grid, the 11 mark in one cell, one service accent on the numeral only.

2. **Let the gap do the talking.** Artemii Lebedev's "Same tools. How do I stand out?" card puts the question at the top of a black card and the answer far below it, with nothing in between. The empty space is the pause before the punchline, so the answer lands harder than any graphic could make it.
   - Check: if you delete everything between the two lines of text, does the frame get better? Then delete it.
   - Ours: Q&A cards for the questions clients actually ask ("can't I just make my logo with AI?"), question in Inter Tight at the top, one arrow line answer low on the card, Bone or Ink, no image. Use it in stories and as the second slide of a carousel.
