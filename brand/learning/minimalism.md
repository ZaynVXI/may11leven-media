# Minimalism principles

One principle per study session. Each one is a rule we can check on a frame, not a mood.

1. **Let the structure be the decoration.** Motto's workbook covers have no image at all: a hairline grid, a title, one big numeral. When a frame feels empty, add a visible system (grid, rule, numbered cell) before adding a picture. If the grid can't carry it, the idea isn't clear yet.
   - Check: could this frame be printed in one colour and still look finished?
   - Ours: Bone ground, ink hairline grid, the 11 mark in one cell, one service accent on the numeral only.

2. **One focus at a time; quiet secondary text.** From the Readymag "minimal portfolio" study (Jacob Youngblood's two text styles, Tiago Canario's image toggle) and the minimal.gallery curator's notes (trust and usefulness over big names). Secondary text sits in one quieter tone and comes forward on hover or focus; only one thing on the page is at full strength.
   - Check: squint. Is there exactly one element at 100% ink plus the accent? Does the quiet tone still pass 4.5:1?
   - Ours: Ink 100% for the active row and the plan, Ink 62% for everything else (the lowest tint that passes on Bone, 4.68:1), Infrared on one object only.
   - Trap found today: our old 45% tint looked elegant and failed contrast (2.94:1). Quiet must still be readable.
