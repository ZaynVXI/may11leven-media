# Minimalism principles

One principle per study session. Each one is a rule we can check on a frame, not a mood.

1. **Let the structure be the decoration.** Motto's workbook covers have no image at all: a hairline grid, a title, one big numeral. When a frame feels empty, add a visible system (grid, rule, numbered cell) before adding a picture. If the grid can't carry it, the idea isn't clear yet.
   - Check: could this frame be printed in one colour and still look finished?
   - Ours: Bone ground, ink hairline grid, the 11 mark in one cell, one service accent on the numeral only.

2. **One weight carries the whole system.** Estudio Repúblicas built RN Arquitectura on a single weight of Basel Classic, one olive hero colour and no symbol at all; Courier Prime appears only in technical documents (the-brandidentity.com). Hierarchy comes from size, position and space, never from bold.
   - Check: switch every weight on the frame to Regular. If the order of reading changes, the layout is leaning on weight instead of scale and space.
   - Ours: the Zanzibar 9.6 s tile uses Inter Display Regular only, from the 400 px numeral to 20 px captions. The one emphasis in the footer ("86%") is colour, not weight.

3. **The quiet object is noticed last.** Minimalissimo's reading of Jasper Morrison: the best objects don't announce themselves; "the balance of how they look and how they perform is correct" (buttondown.com/minimalissimo). The Kehikko chair is named for "framework" and keeps only what the form needs; iA's notebook is all-white with the detail in the guidelines, not the surface. Restraint is a performance test, not a look: strip until removing one more thing would break the job.
   - Check: name the one job of the screen in a sentence. If an element doesn't serve that sentence, it's decoration - cut it.
   - Ours: Mita's one job is "show what the last token actually bought". The hero numeral, the token string and the three-part split serve it; everything else (gradients, cards-within-cards, a second accent) was cut. The green block earns its place because it IS the reading, not a panel around one.

4. **Show the number people would have to ask for.** Most Kigali listings say "From 374,000,000 RWF, check brochure" (The ONE Residence, plutproperties.com). The minimal page isn't the one with the least on it; it's the one that answers the question without a call. Holographik's price ladder (holographik-13) shows eleven values and stays calm because they are one size, one face, and only one is lifted.
   - Check: what would a user have to message you to find out? If it's a price, a slot or a status, put it on the first screen and cut something decorative to make room.
   - Ours: Ikiyaga (practice) lists all eleven flats with prices; status is tone plus a strike-through, not three coloured pills.

5. **Let the sentence be the grid.** On Motto's shelf, the Offporter site (picked by Browsing Mode as Site of the Week) has no hero image at all: its one-line promise is split word by word across sixteen numbered columns, I. to XVI., with empty space above and below. The copy is the layout. When a page feels like it needs more elements, first try spreading the one sentence that matters across the structure.
   - Check: can the frame be explained by one sentence that is also visible on it? If the layout needs a second paragraph to fill space, the grid is wrong, not the copy.
   - Ours: a lodge homepage where the three things a late guest needs (shuttle · check-in · price) sit as numbered columns across the fold, before any photo.

6. **Make "no" as easy as "yes".** Minimal isn't one big button and a faint escape link. Safaricom's Hakikisha used to cancel a wrong payment only if you dialled 1 within 15 seconds; many people didn't understand it. The redesign put the recipient's name first and asked a plain Yes or No, and daily reversal requests fell from about 12,000 to 4,000 (techcabal.com). Two equal choices are calmer than one loud one, because nobody has to hunt for the way out.
   - Check: is the safe choice the same size and as easy to reach as the main one? If the user needs to read small print to back out, the screen is busy, however empty it looks.
   - Ours: the Tuma name check (practice) sets "Not them" and "Yes, send" as two equal halves of one track; the name is the largest type on the card, the amount second.

7. **Say the shared fact once.** Every fresh juice at Smoothie DJS on Lower State House Road costs KES 250 (Uber Eats listing). Our first Miwa draft printed "350 ml" and "KES 250" on all seven rows: fourteen repeats of two facts. The headline now says "Every glass KES 250" once, and the rows spend that space on the one thing that differs, the time each juice was pressed.
   - Check: scan a column. If every cell holds the same value, lift it into a heading and give the column to what changes.
   - Ours: Miwa (practice), rows read name, ingredients, pressed time; price and size live in the headline and the lede.

8. **Draw the thing instead of describing it.** PXDX's tennis app replaces a court list with the club's floor plan (dribbble.com/shots/24603734); the Kenya Railways FAQ says the coach and seat number only arrive in the confirmation after you pay (metickets.krc.co.ke/faqs.php, item 10). A dropdown plus "auto-assigned" rows is four controls that say nothing; one drawing of the coach answers "where will we sit?" before anyone asks.
   - Check: if a choice has a shape in the real world (seats, rooms, plots, shelves), is that shape on the screen? If the user has to imagine it, add the drawing and remove the list.
   - Ours: the coach seat picker (practice): one coach outline, 64 seats, four in the accent, no dropdowns.

9. **Do the sum for them.** A prepaid meter shows units left (12.4 kWh); the question in the user's head is "when will the lights go off?" Our first Mita widget draft printed 12.4 twice, in a big tile and in the sentence, and still left the division to the reader. The final widget says "about 4 days of power at your usual 3.1 kWh a day", and the number lives only in its own tile. Webflow calls the same idea "front-loading essentials without hiding complexity" (webflow.com/blog/web-design-trends-2026). Minimal isn't fewer numbers; it's no arithmetic left for the user.
   - Check: for each number on screen, ask what the user does with it next. If the answer is a calculation, show the result in words and keep the raw number once.
   - Ours: Mita widgets (practice): the sentence carries days and the deadline, the green tile carries kWh, the token tile carries what KES 1,000 bought.
