# Market study: agri-trade and export companies (from the Skynaps reel and site)

## The reel (format study only, never reuse his face or footage)
- About 3 minutes, filmed in a bean warehouse, with handheld B-roll of sacks, grading and weighing.
- Hook: "You do not have to be from East Africa to make money from East Africa." This is the contrarian promise.
- Real numbers early, for credibility: in 2024 Kenya imported about 26,843 t of dry kidney beans, worth about USD 9.1m. Tanzania supplied about 16,279 t and Ethiopia 10,175 t. In Q1 2025, Tanzania supplied about 96.7% of Kenya's maize imports. These are his figures, citing Kenya's AFA; we haven't checked them.
- Then a step list: find buyers first, then go to the farms. Then organise collection, sorting, grading, bagging, warehousing and transport. Then forward supply agreements. Then a warning: prices change and quality problems can wipe out the margin.
- Captions: bold uppercase words burned into the middle third, with orange on the keyword. The video ends on a CTA to download the app.
- Why it works: a strong promise, proof on location, hard numbers, useful steps and one clear CTA.

## The business (skynapsng.com)
- A Nigerian agro export and commodity company, running since 2015, with an app on iOS and Android. Services: export, logistics, aggregation, warehousing, market linkage and a digital linking platform.
- Website gaps:
  - Four of the six service pages are placeholders.
  - No proof: no buyers, volumes or certifications.
  - Inconsistent names and copy errors.
  - Stock photos.
  - No product catalogue or spec sheets.
  - The form needs JavaScript; images have no alt text; no address or phone number.

## Why the segment matters
- Afreximbank forecasts intra-African trade at about USD 230bn in 2026 (+10%). Agri-food plus manufacturing make up about 48-50% of flows (Afreximbank outlook, March 2026).
- Nigerian agro exports have long been turned away partly because of poor packaging and compliance (BusinessDay 2016; the issue is still raised by NEPC and the NSC).
- In trade, trust is the product. A foreign buyer judges you by the website, spec sheet, photos, documents and sack before replying.
