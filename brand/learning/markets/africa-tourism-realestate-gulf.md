# Markets: African tourism and real estate, then the Gulf, then the rest

Zayn's order: **the whole of Africa is our target market**, East Africa's tourism and real estate first among equals, then the Middle East, then the rest of the world, where we think outside the box.

## East Africa: tourism and hospitality (Rwanda, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda)
- **Facts:** W Hospitality Group's 2026 pipeline report (Hospitality Net, March 2026) counts a record 123,846 hotel rooms in Africa's chain pipeline, up 18.6%.
  - Kenya, Tanzania and Ethiopia have some of the highest shares under construction on the continent: Kenya 79.5%, Tanzania 77.5%, Ethiopia 79.9%.
  - 31,768 rooms are slated to open in 2026 and 33,381 in 2027.
  - The big chains (Marriott, IHG, Radisson) hold about 80% of the pipeline.
- **Problem:** independent lodges, boutique hotels, safari camps, guesthouses and tour operators compete with chain marketing budgets. Their direct bookings are lost to OTAs (Booking.com, Expedia) and their sites are slow, dated or desktop-only.
- **Gap:** they can't afford chain agencies, and generic templates make every lodge look the same.
- **Our fix:** brand identity plus a mobile-first booking site (the Inzu concept is our proof), product and room photography direction, Blender visuals for unbuilt rooms, and Instagram content systems.
- **Where to find them:** Rwanda Development Board listings, Kenya Tourism Board, the Tanzania Tourist Board, Uganda Tourism Board, Magical Kenya Travel Expo, Kwita Izina (gorilla naming, Rwanda), Akwaaba (Lagos), safari directories (SafariBookings), Instagram location tags.

## Real estate: developers and agents
- **Facts:** Kenyan, Nigerian and Rwandan press (The Standard, Capital FM, ThisDay, The Guardian Nigeria, The Kenya Times, 2026) report diaspora money driving property demand and off-plan sales. The same press reports scam scandals and buyer distrust ("do your homework on developers").
- **Problem:** off-plan buyers, especially in the diaspora, can't visit, so they must trust renders and websites. Weak visuals and amateur sites read as risk.
- **Gap:** many developers use cheap renders and template sites. Trust signals are missing: progress updates, real site photos, clear pricing, verified approvals.
- **Our fix:** high-fidelity Blender architectural and interior renders (no AI label), a project microsite with a construction-progress page, brochure and identity, a showcase reel, and content for diaspora audiences in London, Houston, Toronto, Dubai and Johannesburg.
- **Cities:** Nairobi (Tatu City, Kilimani, Westlands), Kigali (Kigali Innovation City), Dar es Salaam, Zanzibar, Kampala, Lagos, Accra, Cape Town.
- **The bridge:** diaspora buyers live in our Western markets. One concept can speak to both.

## The rest of Africa (always in view)
- **Nigeria and Ghana:** fashion, beauty, food, fintech, real estate.
- **South Africa and Namibia:** wine, tourism, DTC brands.
- **Morocco and Egypt:** together more than 45% of the hotel pipeline. A bilingual (Arabic and French) brand need.
- **Ethiopia:** coffee export, hotels.
- **Type note:** check client languages with the font lab (Yoruba, Igbo, Hausa, Amharic, Arabic). Brands that show local languages correctly stand out.

## Middle East (second)
- **Facts:** Campaign Middle East (2026) puts the Saudi agency market in the low-to-mid $2bn range, growing about 5–6% a year, driven by digitalisation, tourism and SMEs.
  - SMEs are increasingly using agencies for social, performance and e-commerce work.
  - Briefs are shifting to mid-size, always-on digital work, with measurable outcomes (leads, conversions).
  - There's a talent shortage in Riyadh.
- **Problem:** new hotels, restaurants, clinics and SMEs in Saudi Arabia and the UAE need bilingual Arabic and English brands and content fast. Local senior talent is expensive and scarce.
- **Our fix:** remote bilingual identity and content systems (Readex Pro or IBM Plex Sans Arabic pairings), CGI product visuals, hospitality sites.
- **Bridge:** Gulf investors and operators in African hospitality and real estate (UAE groups are active in East Africa).

## Outside the box (random research, keep adding)
- **Diaspora real estate buyers abroad:** brand and site work for agents who sell African property to diaspora buyers in the UK, US and Canada.
- **Safari and adventure operators in Europe** (UK, Germany, Switzerland) that sell East African trips and need modern brand content.
- **Coffee, tea and cacao importers in Europe** sourcing from Rwanda, Ethiopia, Uganda and Ghana, which tell origin stories with weak visuals (see agri-trade.md).
- **Eco-lodges and conservation NGOs** that need donor-facing sites and reports.
- **Medical tourism clinics** (Nairobi, Kigali, Dubai) that need trust-building brands.
- **International schools** across Africa and the Gulf, which recruit expat families and need premium sites.
