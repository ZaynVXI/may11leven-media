## MotiveOne.com, frame by frame

**Hero.** A dusk-lit 3D warehouse: solar roof, loading bays, chargers, cars, long shadows. Copy sits low-left:
- a mono kicker in caps: "ENERGY, CONNECTIVITY & INTELLIGENCE"
- a three-line grotesk headline: "One partner for your industrial operations."

**Scroll story on the same model.**
- The camera stays on one building while the copy swaps: "Connecting systems across your business", then "Powering more efficient processes".
- Glowing cyan lines trace the power and data routes through the building.
- The roof lifts away (a cutaway) to show forklifts and racks inside.
- One object explains the whole offer. Every service is a layer on that object.

**Abstract chapter.** A black field with orange and cyan light ribbons. Centred copy: "Coming together as one integrated solution", then "Turning your data into intelligence", which lands on a glowing grid floor with a control room. Orange stands for energy and cyan for data, and the two colours carry meaning everywhere on the site.

**Proof chapter.**
- Stacked cards with corner-bracket marks (viewfinder corners) and an arrow-and-counter label "↳ 01/04" above a mono caps label.
- A huge light-weight numeral ("42", "1.2k", "50") with a mono source line, "FROM 2006 TO NOW".
- One small real photo, then a body paragraph in mid-grey.
- Background: a dark warm gradient with fine vertical striping.

**People and community.**
- Real field-worker photography.
- "Giving back to our community." is a centred title over a fanned 3D carousel of photos.
- "Serving customers across the country." sits on white, with a horizontal row of location cards and arrow controls.

**Brand close.** The Motive "M" mark in dark grey, with orange and cyan corner strokes echoing the light lines. The motif loops back.

**Navigation (mobile).**
- A white sheet over the page. A "↳ MENU" mono label, then large grotesk items with small square arrow buttons; → opens a sub-level, ↗ goes to its own page.
- A black "GET STARTED ↗" button pinned at the bottom.
- Search: a live typeahead. Results carry a mono type label (ENERGY SOLUTION / PRODUCT / SERVICE) and the typed letters are greyed inside the match.

**Search panel (desktop).** Before you type, it already shows Industries, Solutions, Popular Products and Popular Services as photo rows with arrows. You never face an empty search box.

**The reel itself (how they post it).**
- Screens float as a single card on a calm sky or on a dark field with light trails, at a slight 3D tilt.
- A slow scroll-through, about 14 to 45 seconds.
- The caption asks for votes in plain, warm words. The voice is personal ("Much love").

## What we take (transform, never copy)
1. **One hero object, many layers.** Build the client's real place in Blender (a hotel, an estate, a warehouse, a clinic) and tell every service as a layer on it: power, water, booking flow, data. No stock hero, no abstract blob.
2. **Colour as meaning.** Give each system a colour (for example energy and money) and keep it on every line, icon, chart and the logo close. Our one-accent rule still holds: one accent for the brand, plus at most one functional colour.
3. **Proof cards.** A step counter (↳ 01/04), a mono label, a giant light numeral, a source line, one real photo, a short paragraph. Numbers must be real and sourced.
4. **Search that is never empty.** An empty state that shows popular routes, plus typeahead with type labels and matched letters. Usable, and a detail juries notice.
5. **Mono for system, grotesk for voice.** Mono caps for labels, counters and buttons; a tight grotesk for headlines. We already do this; Better Off proves it scales to enterprise.
6. **Post the site as a floating card on a real sky or field,** not a flat screenshot. This fits our hi-fi mockup rule.

## Where it fits our services
02 websites (corporate, industrial, real estate), 03 apps (search and menu patterns), 04 packaging and 3D (the hero object), 05 and 06 AI automation and agents ("turning your data into intelligence" chapter as the honest way to show systems).

## Watch out
- Light ribbons and glowing grids read as tech cliché when they are not tied to meaning. Use them only where the colour codes a real system.
- They use real worker photos; we use free licensed photos and never faces from reference material.
