# Motto study (one entry per session, newest last)
Read before brand strategy, naming or identity work. Steal the thinking, never the surface or their trademarked phrases.

## Nomina (formerly Omni Network): one idea that points two ways
- What they did: renamed and rebranded a New York DeFi infrastructure company. The idea "All In" works twice: inside, a demand for focus and conviction; outside, an invitation to builders, investors and users to commit to open finance. Voice: driven, optimistic, approachable. Two named audiences, an early mover who needs credibility and a newcomer who needs simplicity. Rollout covered community, X campaigns and the token migration (wearemotto.com/blog/behind-the-big-idea-nomina).
- Naming: they dropped "Omni" because the word was too common to own. "Nomina" (Latin, "names") keeps the old name's letters and cadence, so existing users still recognise it.
- Visual rules: clean, professional, pragmatic; straight lines, focal points, momentum; playful looks and institutional sameness both rejected. The written case gives no typefaces or hex values.
- Why it works: one sentence does the strategy's work for staff and customers alike, so every later choice (voice, imagery, campaign) can be checked against it. The rename keeps the equity it already had instead of starting from zero.
- Also new: Motto teased a full identity for a company "rethinking how millions improve at the game they love", with 15+ sub-brands under one idea (x.com/wearemotto). Their offer is packaged as FastTrack / Foundation / Flagship (wearemotto.com/services).
- From our refs (workbook-02): the hairline grid is the cover. Title in a merged cell, one big numeral in the corner cell, one colour on near-black.
- Our move: every concept gets a two-way line, one rule for the team and one promise to the guest. For a Zanzibar hotel: "Show the room before the guest waits" (team) and "See it, price it, book it in one place" (guest). For renames in our markets, keep the letters or sound of the old name when people already use it.

## Performance Golf: the idea reframes the chore (Engagement Flagship)
- What they did: rebranded a sports-tech golf-improvement company in a crowded instruction market where quick fixes and pro lessons leave amateurs overwhelmed. The Idea Worth Rallying Around is "Love Your Game": it reframes practice from a chore into the pleasure of visible progress, so the brand sells certainty and enjoyment, not another gadget. The PG1 offer fixes root flaws through a personalised sequence (wearemotto.com/portfolio/performance-golf).
- Voice as system: "part coach, part fan" on three named pillars (Unwavering, Constructive, Uplifting), progress over perfection. The voice pillars are written before the visuals, so copy and imagery can be checked against them.
- Visual: neutral primary palette anchored by one owned accent ("Performance Orange", warmth/passion); a type system meant to read as technical and conversational at once; a data-rich UI that shows diagnostics, drills and gear as one connected ecosystem; cinematic photography on an effort -> breakthrough -> elation arc.
- How they present it: the case study is Overview / Strategy / Identity, each a short paragraph plus a bulleted deliverables list, closing on a client line ("North Star for our teams"). The structure itself argues that strategy comes first.
- Our move: for a utility app in our markets, reframe the chore the same way. Buying prepaid electricity is a grudge errand; the idea for Mita is "know what you're paying for" - turn the opaque token into a visible split (energy vs taxes vs arrears) so the product sells understanding, not just a top-up. One owned accent on a neutral ground, the voice decided before the screen.

## Goodnotes: evolve the equity, add one human mark (Goodnotes 6 launch)
- What they did: rebranded Goodnotes (21M+ monthly users) from an iPad note app into "an AI-powered digital paper company and learning ecosystem" for the Goodnotes 6 launch. Emotional core "the joy of learning"; campaign "Notes Reimagined"; Sage archetype with three voice pillars (curiosity, empowerment, joyfulness). Case structure: Overview, Immersion, Brand Strategy, Verbal Identity, Visual Identity, Activation (wearemotto.com/portfolio/goodnotes).
- The identity move: they kept the old symbol and the primary teal, and added one human element, a "smiling scribble" by Jonathan Mak that runs through the wordmark, illustration and motion. A thick "journey line" is the one graphic device; a graph-paper texture is the only grid (designcompass.org; brandpad.io/goodnotes-guidelines).
- Measured system (public guidelines): Roobert for everything, Goodnotes Hand as the secondary handwritten face, Noto Sans for other scripts. Primary Navy #252A4D, GN Teal #45BFDB / #6DD9F2 / #99ECFF, Ink #111213, Eraser #F9F9F9 / #E6E5E4. Accents (Canary #FFE55F, Fire Engine #EB513C and others) are "used in low ratios". Clear space = the "O" of the wordmark.
- Why it works: users keep what they already recognise (symbol, teal), so the rebrand reads as growth, not a new company. The one hand-drawn element carries the emotion so the rest can stay a calm sans system. Hansberger's line from the interview: "Culture is brand in motion" (the-brandidentity.com); the case lists culture work (values, manifesto) beside the logo.
- Our move: for a rebrand in our markets, keep one owned asset (a colour, a symbol, a sound) and add exactly one human mark from the client's world: a Kinyarwanda imigongo zigzag drawn by hand for a Kigali brand, a Swahili door-carving line for a Lamu hotel. One line, one hand, everything else a quiet sans system.

## Studio DADO: a famous rule, bent one word (flagship refresh)
- What they did: refreshed the brand of an award-winning interior studio known for cruise ships as it moved into hotels on land. Headline of the case: "A brand built at sea sets its sights on land." Research: interviews, workshops, a brand and competitive audit, social listening. They found the studio designs around how people move and feel, not only how rooms look (wearemotto.com/portfolio/studio-dado).
- The idea: "Form Follows Feeling", one word swapped in the most famous rule in design. People already know the original, so the new line is remembered on first read and argues with the whole category in three words.
- Why it works: the problem was winning the first meeting, not the work; a line people can repeat does the selling before the portfolio. Colour moves warm to cool to suggest a range of feelings, with gradients as "emotion in motion"; the logo got softer curves. Photography is real people in real rooms, with small traces like a shadow on a wall or a book on a bed ("spaces lived deeply"). Full verbal kit: voice pillars, persona, word kit, manifesto.
- How they present a brand: overview headline, the tension found in research, the one line, then system pieces (logo, colour, type and grid, art direction, site).
- Our move: for a concept identity, find the one rule everyone in that client's category repeats and bend one word of it into the brand line, then let the photos show traces of people (a cup, a chair moved) rather than people's faces. Our test: a stranger should be able to repeat it after one read. Ours stays plain English and never borrows their trademarked phrases.

## Armitron: the idea is about the moment, not the object (Fullsail rebrand)
- What they did: the first full rebrand in nearly 50 years for Armitron, a 1975 US watch brand sold through Walmart, JCPenney, Kohl's and Amazon whose relevance had faded in big-box retail. Goal: reach younger buyers and grow direct sales. Idea Worth Rallying Around: "Love Every Second", built on the insight that time is a weave of memories and feelings. Voice pillars Positive, Human, Partnering. The logo nods to the 1975 original. Case sections: Overview, Challenge, Immersion (with an insight pull quote), Brand Strategy, Product Strategy, Verbal Identity, Visual Identity, Photoshoot, Brand Launch, Shopify Website (wearemotto.com/portfolio/armitron).
- Also read: Sunnystep, Singapore walking shoes, "Joy Of Movement", tagline "Walk on the Bright Side", black, white and one "Sunkissed Orange" (wearemotto.com/portfolio/sunnystep). Neither case names typefaces or hex values.
- Why it works: a watch brand in a discount aisle can't win on the watch. The line moves the value from the object to what the owner does with the time, which no competitor on the same shelf claims. The case ends with a working shop and a photoshoot, so the idea ships as something people can buy, not a deck.
- Our move: for a product brand in our markets, write the line about the buyer's moment, not the product's features, then prove it in one piece of real UI. Today's Miwa page leads with "Pressed this morning" (the moment) instead of "fresh juice" (the object), and the panel shows the morning's batch running down.

## Cresta: the name already holds the idea (Engagement Flagship, AI)
- What they did: rebranded Cresta, a contact-centre AI platform from Stanford's AI lab, after a USD 125M Series D and the launch of its AI agents. Motto's framing: "A world-class platform hiding behind a second-tier brand." The idea came out of the name itself: the crest of a wave, "the moment where momentum and transformation meet". Three words carry it: transformative, adaptive, flow (wearemotto.com/portfolio/cresta; cresta.com/blog/wave-of-change-introducing-crestas-new-brand).
- The system: a wordmark with a stylised C and a rounded peak on the A, drawn from Lissajous curves (the harmonic shapes of signals, standing for dialogue); the first standalone symbol, overlapping lines that hint at the C; the logo animates. They kept some of the old ocean blues and added a brighter range. Type is reported differently: Motto names Diatype (Dinamo), Cresta's own post names DM Sans with a Mono; we don't know which is final. No hex values published. Scope included accessibility and contrast testing, a motion pack and a pitch deck.
- How they present it: Overview (a metadata strip: Flagship, Rebrand, AI, B2B) then Challenge, Immersion, Symbol, Color and Type, Testimonial, Applications, Press quote. The client line: Motto "honored where we've been, while bringing to life the vision we have for the future."
- Why it works: the company already owned a word with a picture in it. Motto didn't invent a metaphor; they dug the one out of the name and turned it into geometry (a real curve from signal maths), so the mark is both the wave and the product (conversation as a signal). Keeping the old blues is the same equity rule as Goodnotes.
- Skip: their imagery leans on generative-AI backgrounds and AI-made portraits. We never do that.
- Our move: before any naming or identity sketch, write down what the client's existing name already pictures (Kusi = the south-east monsoon wind; Mita = meter), and draw the mark from the real physics or object behind it (a wind rose, a meter's dial), not from a mood. Then show the case Motto's way: a metadata strip first, the tension in one line, the symbol, then colour and type.
