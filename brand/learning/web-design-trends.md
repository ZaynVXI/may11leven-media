# web.design.trends study (Instagram, 85K): beautiful AND useful
Refs: brand/refs/web-design-trends/ (3 grid screenshots + Axon Labs contact sheets). Zayn's rule: study these pages often; beauty is not enough, every piece must solve a problem.
Source note: the account reposts work, mostly concept shots, often without a link. Get the creator from the post caption or tags before quoting a name. Monumental (robotics start-up that automates on-site construction) was an Awwwards nominee in Feb 2024, with an average of about 7.2 from the five visible jury scores (awwwards.com).

## Axon Labs deep dive (fintech data concept; the best "useful" example here)
- The headline is the problem, in plain words: "Make complex data human readable", "Axon starts where spreadsheets stop".
- The product is explained as a diagram, not a screenshot: five isometric boxes (CRM, Banking, Payroll, Billing, ERP) joined by orange lines, with "insight" tags that pop up as you scroll.
- A numbered left rail ([01 // UNIFY] … [05 // TRACE]) stays put while the diagram changes. The user always knows where they are and how far is left (02 / 05).
- Process as three cards: Ingest, Attribute, Connect. A verb, one sentence and a tiny animation each.
- Proof: a strip of logos for the tools it connects to (Xero, Sage, HubSpot, Stripe), then a customer quote with two outcome numbers (12 unprofitable accounts found in week one; 18% of margin recovered in two quarters). The quote and numbers look invented for the concept, so never copy figures.
- Low-risk CTA everywhere: "Book a call" for a 20-minute walkthrough, plus "Request demo" in the header.
- System: light grey #E4E4E4-ish, near-black and ONE orange; wide extended grotesk in caps for headings; small mono labels; hazard stripes for the transition. That's the whole identity.
- Brand object: one hero 3D object (an orange "TP-X9" power-switch box with cables) that stands for "plug your data in". It's reused in the hero, transitions and the reels.
- Presentation: a laptop hung from climbing ropes and carabiners (the prop matches the idea of holding everything together), plus flat screens on a dark canvas.

## Other patterns in the grid
- Scroll as a configurator: "Scroll to fit the kitchen", "Scroll to build your backyard", "Scroll into sanctuary". Scrolling builds the product in place. This is the strongest pattern for real estate, interiors and lodges.
- Data made friendly: an energy app ("See exactly what your energy is doing", "Let the sun do the paperwork") uses illustrated isometric worlds plus small stat cards. A nutrition site puts AI-scan data on top of a photo.
- Product as the hero on a neutral background: a bottle, a robot arm ("AI-enhanced high-energy"), engine parts ("The atom, twice unlocked"). One object, lots of air.
- Big editorial type: SANTAL across the full width, "Ready for monumental TV?", sneakers in condensed caps. The type is the image.
- Cinematic photo plus serif: Collison, Themyscira, a "You Believed" script, a hay-themed beauty site. Quiet luxury for hospitality and beauty.
- Utility as the hero: a world-clock site whose hero is the live time (08:59), with cities below.
- How they sell on IG: every site is shown on a device placed in the brand's own world (autumn leaves for a delivery brand, concrete for an architect, ropes for "connect"). The mockup tells the story before you read a word.

## How we use it
- Every concept: write the problem chain first (may11leven-problem-first), then a headline that names the problem in the client's words.
- Explain the service or product as a diagram (isometric boxes or a flow) with a numbered rail, not a wall of text. Our own site can show "How 11 days works" this way.
- One accent colour, one display face plus mono, and one hero object per brand that carries through the site, reels and packaging.
- Proof block on every site: the tools it connects to (M-Pesa, WhatsApp, Stripe, Booking.com), plus real outcomes only, with a source.
- Low-risk CTA: "book a 20-minute call" or "comment 11"; the WhatsApp button stays visible.
- Present each concept on a device inside a scene from its own world (a lodge deck, a coffee sack, a site office), never on a plain gradient.
- Steal the structure, not the look (steal-like-an-artist.md). Run the sameness check and the preflight before posting.
