# Keeping the work human (from HUMAN.md, a designer's manifesto; paraphrased)

Source: https://www.human-md.ai/ (Briana May). Our takeaways, in our words:

1. Know the rules of a craft, then break one on purpose and be able to say why.
2. Look for ideas outside the usual places (not only Dribbble and Pinterest): the client's street, their objects, old print, science, other crafts.
3. Keep the real person who uses the work in view in every brief.
4. Aim for a feeling, not only correctness.
5. Make room for delight; work that was fun to make shows it.
6. Try a lot, keep little. Discard freely.
7. We own every output, even when tools helped. "The tool made it" is never an excuse.
8. Iterate by hand on what tools give us; never ship the first result.
9. Be both the maker and the thinker behind the idea.

How it maps to our process: point 1 and 4 belong in the judgement brief; 2 in product research; 6 and 8 in the quality gate (no first drafts ship); 7 is our standing rule that nothing goes out unchecked.
