# Study notes: 19–24 Nov 2026

## Motto: Brand Lab, Agent Hawkeye, Docker "Run on Trust"
**What we saw.** Motto set up a Brand Lab, a team for experiments and new tools. Its first public tool is **Agent Hawkeye**, an AI that checks design work. The reel shows it as a small white panel with a black icon. Code runs inside it (a brand audit of a Figma project), then cards appear for each check: "Validating Proportions" (the logo's construction circles), "Analyzing Typography" (an "Aa" specimen), "Confirming Grid System". It ends with a single pill: "Identity Validated". Separately, Motto's Docker rebrand is built on one idea, "Run on Trust". The whale is redrawn on a construction grid and shown as a keyboard key, a sticker on a blue notebook, a framed poster, an app icon with a notification badge, and "] TRUSTED" bracket labels.
**What we take.**
- QA can be the show. A design check shown step by step (proportions → type → grid → validated) proves rigour better than any claim. Our preflight already does this; we should show it in posts.
- One idea, carried everywhere. "Run on Trust" is one sentence that every object repeats. Every product of ours needs its one sentence before any pixel.
- Their AI story is about trust and control ("giving AI agents the freedom to act while keeping them secure and under control"). That's the same story as our After hours guardrails.
**Steal, don't copy.** No whale, no "] TRUSTED" brackets, no Hawkeye panel look. We'll build our own visible QA step (a "proof strip": each check as a tick on a measured line).

## Artemii Lebedev: portfolio site and Awwwards Site of the Day
The site won Awwwards Site of the Day and the Developer Award (shown as posters drifting in wind over a glitch-pixel night scene, red then purple). The site itself is a black field with small white text windows ("browser windows" in a stack). Each project opens as a window cluster: a text column with a dense index, one big image (a taxi at night, a laptop on a stage of cables), and a blue UI tile. Projects layer on top of each other like a desktop.
**What we take.** The layout is mostly text index and windows, and the images do the talking. It's dense, but every element is small and aligned to one grid. Award announcements become their own designed object (the posters in wind), not a screenshot of a badge.

## Qclay: Paylane
A fintech app: blue gradient header, white sheet with the balance ($117,450.17, cents in grey), Pay and Request buttons, transactions, small bar charts, a "Since last week your income has grown!" onboarding screen, and a home-screen widget. Clean and well built.
**What we take, and what we avoid.** The grey cents and the widget are good small details. But the blue gradient plus big-balance pattern is everywhere in fintech, and the caption ("We are open for new projects 👋 … contact details in BIO or DM") is generic. This is exactly the founder's critique of our own work: polished, not distinct. Lesson: start from a sharp idea specific to the product (Term's school colours and Fridays), then design.

## Zajno: motion guide and the Zettafleet site
**motion.zajno.com** is Zajno's free guide to UI motion: easing, offset and delay, fade, transform and morph, masking, dimension, parallax and zoom. Each section has examples and classic-animation frames. Its point: motion should guide attention and carry the brand, never decorate.
**Zettafleet** (the site and identity in the Zajno videos): a dot-matrix halftone field on black, white "paper" panels with crop marks at the corners, line-drawn isometric cubes for each step (data processing → post-training → continued pre-training → pre-training), "Our principle 01 / 04" counters, and bracket corners around words ("⌜your data⌟ into a state-of-the-art ⌜AI model⌟"). Merch carries the same brackets on a black hoodie; badges, a business card in a hand, and a reception wall.
**What we take.**
- Motion built from one primitive: the dot field morphs, the cubes draw in line by line, panels slide with crop marks. One system, many moments.
- Isometric line icons for a process are clearer than photos.
- Crop marks and counters make a web page feel like a printed spec. That's useful for our case-study PDFs, but the brackets and dot-matrix are theirs and .raw's, so we use neither.

## Holographik: nine videos and holographik.co
The videos show one generative identity system across every surface. Circles and lines are its only parts. There's a black grid of circles that fill and empty like a sequencer, dials labelled A, B and C, flower shapes made of circles that change form on each page, horizontal bars that swell into diamond waveforms, and documents with spec tables ("Measurements by Study"). They're shown on a phone held in black gloves, on curved displays, and on printed pages turning.
The site: numbered sections (I Principles, II Services, III Process, IV Awards, V Contact), short declarative principle headings ("We lead." "Nothing hidden."), work as a filterable table (#, project, industry, services, year), a showreel, and a persistent "Get in touch".
**What we take.**
- A system beats a logo. Two primitives (circle, line) plus rules produce endless on-brand frames. Our next identity should ship as a small generator, not a static mark.
- Show the system working: the same shapes animating across phone, print and screen in one reel.
- Principles as short sentences. Our version: "The model talks. The system decides." "Nothing hidden." is theirs; ours could be "We show the failure plan."

## NN/g psychology study guide (now a study site)
Eleven areas: attention, Gestalt, memory, sensemaking, decision making, motor, motivation, biases, persuasion, emotion, attitudes to technology. The ones that change our work most:
- **Peak–end rule:** people remember the most intense moment and the end. Our carousels need a strong peak (the loop) and a designed last slide, never a plain contact card.
- **Recognition over recall, chunking, cognitive load:** one idea per slide; show options, don't make people remember.
- **Information scent:** labels must say exactly where they lead ("See the 9.6 s test", not "Learn more").
- **Trust and credibility:** clear identity, polish, real contact details; delay asking for sensitive data. For agents, the **ELIZA effect** means we must set honest expectations: say it's an assistant, say what it can't do.
- **Honest scarcity and no deceptive patterns:** never fake urgency in a booking flow.
- **Aesthetic–usability effect:** beauty hides usability problems, so test usability before polishing.
Full guide: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/psychology-study-guide/

## IxDF (Interaction Design Foundation), now a study site
A large free library: topic definitions, articles and encyclopedia chapters. The framework we'll use is the **five dimensions of interaction design**: words, visuals, physical space, time and behaviour. Check every prototype on all five (we usually forget time and behaviour). The course content behind its paywall isn't for us.

## Luma: "The AI Playbook for Creative Operations" (the PDF, 3 pages)
The argument: the bottleneck is execution at brand standard, not ideas. Revision cycles turn into "approvals theater", and senior people end up doing production QA. The shift it argues for is from prompting to directing: brief and intent, iteration as the core loop, and references and feedback to prevent drift. The creative director becomes a "systems director".
**What we take.** It's the same lesson as Hawkeye and our skill library: write the brand down as a system (references, rules, checks) so every output is judged against it. Luma's own product generates AI images and video, which our no-AI-imagery rule rules out for posts. So we use Luma for ideas only, never for output.

## Lummi
Lummi's library is AI-generated stock images (HubSpot and Codrops describe it as an AI stock image site). **Not installed.** It breaks the rule that nothing we post could earn an AI label. We may browse it for mood and composition only, never download for use.

## BairesDev (Crunchbase)
Customers, not funding, measure success. Don't underprice: it "more often signals uncertainty rather than good value". Hire only the top 1% and compete on delivery quality. Automate the back office (hiring and matching) so growth doesn't need equal overhead. For us: published prices that aren't the cheapest, a quality bar that is visible (preflight, red-team reports), and our own automation behind the studio.

## Not May11Leven: the faceless GTA 6 clipping guide
The PDF "Build a Faceless Clipping Page Run by Grok Bot" and its video are for the GTA channel, not the studio. The useful parts: the payoff-first edit formula, the send test, verifying footage is really GTA 6 and not GTA V, crediting every streamer, and the 2-hour analyse → learn → scout → cut loop with hard rules (never pay, never message anyone, never change the profile without asking). We don't copy one part of it: the daily "warmup" of liking posts so the account looks human. That's platform manipulation, and it puts the account at risk.
