# Artemii Lebedev: the second reference after Motto

## How he presents a website (most of his grid)
- **A flat screen over a blurred photo from the client's own world.** Seafood site over a shrimp farm, student housing over a bedroom, a video app over a city skyline, Edge Manufacturing over its machines. There's no device most of the time. The screen covers about 80% of the width, sits centred or just below centre, and has no shadow and no frame.
- **The background picks the colour.** Each tile has one dominant hue from the scene (cobalt, amber, sage, black), and the site echoes it with one accent (Edge's yellow, Cantor8's cobalt, a green on dark). Everything else is neutral.
- **Phone screens:** flat rounded rectangles, staggered, floating on a solid field in the brand's darkest colour.
- **When he does use a device,** it's real hardware in a real material scene (a laptop on dark stone, a phone in a hand, an iPad on chrome tubes).

## His own brand
- Mono uppercase type, index lists (year · client · industry), a live clock, "4 ongoing projects", a commissions email. It reads like a working document, not an ad.
- A recurring series, "Designer's Inner Voice", numbered D.Vol: one white card on a texture, with a short thought. That's his thought-leadership format.
- Line: "Make meaning, not noise." / "Clarity beats volume."

## Our rules from him
1. Present every website as a slow scroll recording, flat, over a soft-focus photo from the client's own world. The photo must be part of the story (Kusi House: the dhow you arrive on).
2. One accent per project, taken from the scene. Grade every photo into one colour world before it touches the layout.
3. Start a numbered thought series of our own (like D.Vol), in our own visual form, never a copy.

First applied: `concepts/kusi-house/` (kusi-loop.mp4).

## Second study: 11 reels and his year in review
- **One project, many posts.** 21OAKS (student housing) appears at least 8 times, each a different presentation:
  - the desktop site over a blurred real bedroom
  - the desktop site over moving drone or library footage
  - phones staggered on the brand's lilac
  - phones on near-black
  - split frames of lilac and real photos
  - the brand's hand-drawn line illustrations alone (a bed, a lamp, a window drawn as one continuous line; isometric rooms)
  - a numbered room browser (001–013)
  - an aerial drone hero

  Depth beats breadth. A project becomes a whole series.
- **Backgrounds move.** Behind the screen there's often real footage (a library with people, a drone over the property, mist moving through leaves), not a still. The screen scrolls slowly on top.
- **Phones in matched sets.**
  - Shrimpies' phone sits in a striped shirt pocket (a fisherman's shirt for a seafood brand).
  - Cantor8's phone lies on cobalt paper under hard light.
  - IOH's phone lies over a person sleeping (mental health).

  The scene is the brand's metaphor.
- **One signature component per product,** e.g. A Better Lou's "Your clock is ticking" biological-age dial (64), and Ponder's 4-step rail (Ingest, Brief, Generate, Export).
- **A year-in-review series:** black label chips in the top-left ("March", "Diamond Rose Sanctuary"). Each month gets its best project in one frame, which recaps the year and proves output.
- **His own brand illustration and line work** carry warmth into otherwise strict, minimal UI.

## Third study: Cantor8 and "Between Memory and Form"
- **Cantor8 as a whole system:** one cobalt everywhere. The pixel-block mark breaks apart into dividers and transitions, and circuit lines link the sections. The type pairing board (Neue Montreal + Fragment Mono) credits the designer, foundry, release date and glyph count. Around 8 presentations:
  - a laptop in a dark studio
  - a collage of pages on navy
  - screens over a skyscraper shot from below
  - a phone on cobalt paper
  - a laptop on a blue sculpted surface
  - a "Discover the full project on Behance" end card
- **Self-initiated exploration:** one visual (a flower under CRT scanlines), one strict mono layout, repeated over four images. Each card floats over a blurred, enlarged copy of its own image. A dark phone-in-hand shot. Caption: "Visual and typography exploration before the next website drop." Sharing the exploration builds anticipation and shows thinking.
- **Applied:** Kusi type pairing board (credits from the licences, with what each face is used for); Kusi phones staggered on the mangrove-dark field.

## Fourth study: measured, bit by bit (numbers from his frames vs our first report)
| Measure | Lebedev / Cantor8 | Our report v1 | Rule now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dominant colour share | 67–85% one saturated hue (cobalt), or a full photograph | 85–91% pale bone | Commit: a saturated field, or a real photo, or ink. Never a pale page without imagery. |
| Contrast (std of luminance) | 65–86 on photo/UI frames; 45 on the type board | 31–48 | Push contrast: ink on red, bone on ink. |
| Outer edge | ~1.7–3% of width for labels and hairline rules | 5.2% uniform | Tight 32 px edges for labels and rules. Content sits on a column. |
| Grid | Asymmetric: a label column (~26% width) and content from there | Centred, symmetric | 280 px label column; content starts at 312 px. |

## Fifth study: "Designer's Inner Voice", D.Vol. 4 (7 slides, measured)
- **Canvas** 1440×1800 (4:5). One card, 672×900 (3:4), centred: 47% of the width, 50% of the height. Radius about 6 px. Pure black (#010101) and light grey (#F1F1F1), no other colour.
- **Grid:** four hairlines cross at the card's corners, with a dot at each crossing. The card sits 16 px inside that cell, so it looks placed on a drawing board.
- **Cover is inverted:** a light card on black. Inside slides are black cards on light grey.
- **Type:** one neo-grotesque with tight tracking. Two sizes only: about 60 px for the doubt, 34 px for the answer. Text inset is 32 px. The answer always sits on the same line, the card's vertical middle, so swiping feels like one card flipping.
- **Copy, word for word:**
  - Cover: "Designer's Inner Voice". "Fear of stillness. / Flow of creation." Footer: date and "D.Vol. 4".
  - "What if I'm burned out?" → "Don't stare at ashes, light a new fire."
  - "Work feels boring." → "Boredom is training. Use it."
  - "I hate the result." → "Stop chasing fireworks, enjoy sparks."
  - "What if I'm stuck?" → "Better stuck in motion than lying still."
  - "And if no one sees?" → "You're not doing it for 'no one.' You're doing it for yourself."
  - "Perfection?" → "Ship raw, refine later."
- **The pattern:** a doubt in the reader's own voice (1–5 words), then "→" and a one-line answer (4–8 words). Six doubts, one cover. No logo, no CTA, no image. The voice is the reader's, not the studio's.
- **Why it works:** it costs almost nothing to make, reads at thumbnail size, gets saved and shared, and builds a numbered series people come back to.

### Our version (never his card, grid or name)
- **Series:** "Second Thoughts". The doubts a business owner has before hiring a studio, each answered in one line. One service per issue (No. 01 Websites, then Branding, Apps, 3D, Automation, AI agents, Content).
- **Form:** our own system, not a card on a grid. A full-bleed field, a hairline header with ■ markers, the 26% label column, Instrument Serif for the doubt and Inter Tight for the answer. Cover on Infrared, inside on Ink. No dates.
- **Last slide** is the only selling slide: the service and "Comment 11".
